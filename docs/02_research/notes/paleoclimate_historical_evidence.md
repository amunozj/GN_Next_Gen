Target-problem shorthand used throughout:

## 1. Bayesian hierarchical paleoclimate reconstructions (BARCAST, Tingley et al. 2012, Li-Nychka-Ammann 2010, Berliner/Wikle process-data-parameter framework)

### Takeaway
The paleoclimate BHM literature gives the template the GN problem needs. It has three parts. First, a *process model* for the latent field with simple parametric space-time dependence. Second, separate *forward data models* per data type (instrumental, each proxy type), each with its own calibration parameters and noise. Third, *parameter priors* that let sparse eras borrow strength. Posterior draws (an ensemble) are the product, not a point estimate. Multi-resolution data are handled by writing each data model as a function, possibly a time-average, of the shared latent process. BARCAST's own authors note it works well "if the assumptions are not strongly violated". That is the central caveat for the sparse Maunder Minimum (MM).

### Cited Findings
- BARCAST (Bayesian Algorithm for Reconstructing Climate Anomalies in Space and Time) reconstructs a climate field over space and time from a mix of instrumental records and proxies. Part I (development, paleoclimate applications) is J. Climate 23(10):2759–2781. Part II (comparison with RegEM) is J. Climate 23(10):2782–2800 — [Tingley & Huybers 2010a PDF](https://phuybers.sites.fas.harvard.edu/Doc/Tingley_JC2010a.pdf); [Tingley & Huybers 2010b PDF](https://phuybers.sites.fas.harvard.edu/Doc/Tingley_JC2010b.pdf); [PSU record](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/a-bayesian-algorithm-for-reconstructing-climate-anomalies-in-spac/)
- BARCAST's approach is to "specify simple parametric forms for the spatial covariance and temporal evolution of the climate field, along with observation equations linking data types to the true field". If BARCAST's assumptions "are not strongly violated", then in practice-like scenarios it produces field and spatial-mean reconstructions that are more skillful than RegEM's, as measured by the coefficient of efficiency (CE) — [Tingley & Huybers 2010b](https://phuybers.sites.fas.harvard.edu/Doc/Tingley_JC2010b.pdf)
- BARCAST's structure is as follows. The process level is an AR(1) in time with exponentially decaying spatial covariance of the innovations. Each data type (instrumental vs. proxy) gets a linear observation equation (proxy = β1·T + β0 + noise) with type-specific parameters and noise variances. Conjugate priors allow a Gibbs/Metropolis sampler that returns posterior draws of the full field — [Tingley & Huybers 2010a](https://phuybers.sites.fas.harvard.edu/Doc/Tingley_JC2010a.pdf) [structure from domain knowledge of the paper; equations not re-read this session]
- Werner & Tingley extended BARCAST to time-uncertain proxies, so that age-depth model uncertainty is sampled jointly within the hierarchical reconstruction — [Werner & Tingley 2015, Clim. Past 11, 533](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/11/533/2015/cp-11-533-2015.pdf)
- Li, Nychka & Ammann (2010, JASA 105:883–895, doi:10.1198/jasa.2010.ap09379) built a Bayesian hierarchical model that estimates past temperature from proxies with *different temporal resolutions* (tree rings, pollen, boreholes) together with external forcings (solar, volcanic, GHG) as drivers. It gives a rigorous uncertainty quantification. The authors evaluated it in a simulation experiment in which a GCM run is the "true" climate and synthetic proxies are generated from it — [UCAR record](https://data.ucar.edu/sq/dataset/the-value-of-multiproxy-reconstruction-of-past-climate); [WUSTL record](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/the-value-of-multiproxy-reconstruction-of-past-climate/)
- In a JASA discussion, Nychka & Li argue that a hierarchical model is a better solution to the reconstruction problem than direct regression on the proxies — [arXiv 1105.0519 (discussion of McShane & Wyner)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1105.0519)
- Tingley, Craigmile, Haran, Li, Mannshardt & Rajaratnam (2012), "Piecing together the past: statistical insights into paleoclimatic reconstructions", QSR 35:1–22. This statistical review argues for hierarchical models with explicit *forward* (proxy-given-climate) data models rather than inverse regression. It also argues for modeling temporal and spatial dependence explicitly and for propagating parameter uncertainty into reconstruction ensembles — [doi:10.1016/j.quascirev.2012.01.012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.quascirev.2012.01.012) [DOI from domain knowledge, not re-fetched]
- The general "data model / process model / parameter model" hierarchy and its link to data assimilation is set out in Wikle & Berliner (2007), "A Bayesian tutorial for data assimilation", Physica D 230:1–16 — [doi:10.1016/j.physd.2006.09.017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physd.2006.09.017). Berliner (2003), "Physical-statistical modeling in geophysics", JGR 108(D24) — [doi:10.1029/2002JD002865](https://doi.org/10.1029/2002JD002865) [both DOIs from domain knowledge, not re-fetched]
- Pseudoproxy tests of BARCAST have been run on synthetic *long-memory* target fields. Earlier work generated stochastic targets from BARCAST's own AR(1) process equations, a "perfect-model" setup — [Nilsen, Werner & Divine 2018, Clim. Past 14, 947](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/14/947/2018/cp-14-947-2018.pdf)
- A BHM (with Werner and Smerdon as coauthors) was used to reconstruct European summer temperatures since Roman times — [Luterbacher et al. 2016, ERL, per search metadata](https://is.muni.cz/publication/1341254) (only metadata seen)

### Inferences
- **(a) Core model, translated to GN.** BARCAST and Li et al. map directly onto the following three levels.
  - *Process:* `log λ_t = μ + c(t) + η_t`. Here `c(t)` is a quasi-periodic cycle component (for example, a GP with a quasi-periodic kernel and ~11-yr period with variable amplitude). `η_t` is an AR(1)/OU term at monthly or 27-day resolution. `G_t | λ_t ~ NegBin` (day-to-day overdispersion).
  - *Data:* one forward model per observer, `Y_{i,t} | G_t, θ_i(t)`, plus one per proxy (see Q2 and Q4).
  - *Parameters:* `θ_i ~` population distribution (partial pooling by era, instrument and region), drift variance `σ²_drift,i`, overdispersion, and so on.
  - BARCAST's "one observation equation per data type with its own β and noise" is exactly "one detection/threshold model per observer".
- **(a) Multi-resolution data.** Li et al. combine proxies at different temporal resolutions by writing coarse proxies as functions of time-aggregates of the latent process. For GN, yearly statements, monthly means, and published annual means become observation operators that integrate the daily latent `G_t`. Daily drawings remain day-level likelihood terms.
- **(c) Mapping of the "borrow strength" logic.** During the MM, overlap between observers is minimal, so observer thresholds are poorly identified from overlap alone. In a BHM, `θ_i` for an MM observer is shrunk toward the population distribution of thresholds (informed by later, well-overlapped observers with similar instruments). This replaces the ad hoc "daisy-chain" calibration through a sparse overlap network.
- **(c) Time uncertainty.** Werner & Tingley's time-uncertain proxies correspond to historical date errors in GN. Examples are Julian vs. Gregorian dating and transcription errors in the Hoyt & Schatten (1998) database (see Q3). Date uncertainty can be a latent per-record offset with a discrete prior over plausible shifts.
- **(d) Pitfalls.** (i) BARCAST's skill claim holds only when the parametric assumptions hold. An AR(1)/stationary process prior fitted to post-1750 cycles may systematically pull MM activity toward "normal" cycles, which is overconfidence by prior misspecification. (ii) In the MM, the posterior can be prior-dominated. Report prior-vs-posterior contraction per year. (iii) If the process prior contains a "grand minimum" regime whose hyperparameters were informed by isotope records, variant A is no longer sunspot-only (circularity; see Q5).
- **(e) Software and scale.** BARCAST used custom Gibbs samplers. Werner's and others' later BARCAST work is in MATLAB/R [domain knowledge]. For JAX/NumPyro, the model is a state-space model. Use a reduced-resolution latent (monthly or Carrington-rotation, about 5,000 latent values for 1610–2025) with day-level likelihoods that marginalize day-to-day noise analytically, sampled by NUTS. Alternatively, use a Kalman-style marginalization of a linear-Gaussian latent on log scale with a Laplace/EP approximation. Schanner et al. (2025; Q4) show that a comparable-size solar GP model runs with NumPyro NUTS on JAX.

- Full texts of Tingley et al. (2012) QSR and Li et al. (2010) JASA were not fetched. Model equations are given from domain knowledge of those papers, without page-level verification.
- The Werner, Luterbacher & Smerdon (2013, J. Climate) pseudoproxy BARCAST-vs-CCA paper was not retrieved by search, so its findings are not reported here.

## 2. Data assimilation for reconstructions (Last Millennium Reanalysis, offline EnKF, proxy system models, PRYSM)

### Takeaway
The Last Millennium Reanalysis (LMR) is the strongest operational precedent for the "proxy system model per data source plus ensemble posterior" design. It uses an offline ensemble square-root Kalman filter with a climatological prior from a model simulation. Statistical PSMs are calibrated per proxy, with error variance set to the regression residual. 25% of proxies are withheld in each of 51 Monte Carlo realizations, giving a 5,100-member grand ensemble. The PSM paradigm (Evans et al. 2013; PRYSM) splits each proxy's forward model into sensor, archive and observation submodels. That split maps cleanly onto historical sunspot observers and onto 14C and 10Be.

### Cited Findings
- LMR update (Tardif et al. 2019, Clim. Past 15, 1251–1273): offline ensemble square-root Kalman filter (EnSRF). Prior states are random draws from a pre-existing simulation, and the *same* draws are used for every year. Proxies are assimilated serially, and proxy estimates are held in an "appended state" — [Tardif et al. 2019](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/15/1251/2019/)
- The authors justify offline assimilation by the limited skill of model forecasts over the 1-year update interval — [Tardif et al. 2019](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/15/1251/2019/)
- The prior is the CCSM4 Last Millennium simulation (850–1850 CE), using anomalies from the simulation-wide mean, so it "contains no time-specific information such as volcanic events or 20th-century trends" — [Tardif et al. 2019](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/15/1251/2019/)
- PSMs are univariate or bivariate linear regressions calibrated on GISTEMP v4 (temperature) and GPCC v6 (precipitation). Seasonality comes from metadata, or is otherwise chosen by maximizing adjusted R². Only proxies with ≥25 years of overlap with calibration data are used — [Tardif et al. 2019](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/15/1251/2019/)
- Observation-error covariance R is diagonal, with each proxy's error variance equal to its PSM regression residual variance — [Tardif et al. 2019](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/15/1251/2019/)
- 75% of proxies are assimilated and 25% withheld in each Monte Carlo realization. The setup is 100 ensemble members × 51 realizations = a 5,100-member "grand ensemble", with results reported as the grand-ensemble mean — [Tardif et al. 2019](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/15/1251/2019/)
- Localization uses a Gaspari–Cohn function with a 25,000 km cut-off, and skill peaks for radii of 15,000–25,000 km — [Tardif et al. 2019](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/15/1251/2019/)
- Temporal mismatch: reconstructions are annual, while seasonal PSMs are handled by precomputing proxy estimates from seasonally averaged model output. Monthly state variables were avoided because of cost and noisier prior covariances — [Tardif et al. 2019](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/15/1251/2019/)
- The LMR framework and first results are in Hakim et al. (2016), JGR-Atmospheres 121:6745 — [doi:10.1002/2016JD024751](https://doi.org/10.1002/2016JD024751) [DOI from domain knowledge, not re-fetched]
- Proxy system modeling (Evans et al. 2013, QSR 76:16–28) splits a proxy forward model into a *sensor* model (environment → sensor response), an *archive* model (how the signal is stored and smoothed), and an *observation* model (measurement, sampling and dating) — [doi:10.1016/j.quascirev.2013.05.024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.quascirev.2013.05.024) [DOI from domain knowledge, not re-fetched]
- PRYSM is an open-source Python framework implementing PSMs (sensor/archive/observation) for several proxy classes, especially oxygen-isotope systems (Dee et al. 2015, JAMES 7:1220–1247) — [doi:10.1002/2015MS000447](https://doi.org/10.1002/2015MS000447) [DOI from domain knowledge, not re-fetched]
- Time-averaged observations can be assimilated by building the time-averaging into the observation operator (Steiger et al. 2014, J. Climate 27:426–441) — [doi:10.1175/JCLI-D-12-00693.1](https://doi.org/10.1175/JCLI-D-12-00693.1) [DOI from domain knowledge, not re-fetched]
- Mechanistic PSMs can be given Bayesian parameter estimation. An example is VS-Lite for tree-ring width (Tolwinski-Ward et al. 2011, Clim. Dyn., [doi:10.1007/s00382-010-0945-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00382-010-0945-5); Bayesian calibration in Tolwinski-Ward et al. 2013, Clim. Past 9:1481, [doi:10.5194/cp-9-1481-2013](https://doi.org/10.5194/cp-9-1481-2013)) [DOIs from domain knowledge, not re-fetched]
- The `cfr` Python package (Zhu et al. 2024, GMD 17:3409–3431, doi:10.5194/gmd-17-3409-2024) provides a general climate field reconstruction framework. It covers processing proxies, model simulations and instrumental data, calibrating PSMs, running LMR (offline EnKF) and GraphEM, and validating reconstructions — [PyPI cfr](https://pypi.org/project/cfr/2024.4.30); [ISI listing](https://www.isi.edu/results/publications/13884/cfr-v2024-1-26-a-python-package-for-climate-field-reconstruction)

### Inferences
- **(a)/(c) "PSM for each sunspot observer" (Evans decomposition).**
  - *Sensor:* telescope aperture and optics, seeing, and the observer's visual acuity give a threshold on group area or contrast. Formally, observer `i` sees a group of area `A` with probability `p_i(A) = Φ((log A − log θ_i(t))/s_i)`. A hard threshold is the limit `s_i → 0`; that is the ADF threshold concept made generative.
  - *Archive:* the recording medium. Daily drawings, a notebook tally, a letter summarizing weeks or months, or a published yearly statement. This sets the temporal aggregation and *which* information survives, so blanket statements are an archive-level coarsening.
  - *Observation:* modern compilation. Hoyt & Schatten transcription, date errors, and the compiler's interpretation of generic statements (see Q3).
  - Writing each level explicitly separates "observer saw less" (sensor) from "observer wrote less" (archive) from "compiler mis-entered" (observation). Threshold-only calibration conflates these three.
- **(a)/(c) PSMs for isotopes (variant B).**
  - *14C sensor:* production `Q14(φ, M_dipole)`.
  - *14C archive:* carbon-cycle box model plus tree-ring growth season (ticktack; Q4).
  - *14C observation:* AMS measurement error and ring-count dating.
  - *10Be sensor:* production `Q10(φ, M, latitude)`.
  - *10Be archive:* atmospheric transport and deposition, snow accumulation, ice-core chronology.
  - *10Be observation:* AMS and sampling resolution.
- **(a) Offline EnKF vs. full Bayes.** LMR's prior is a *climatological* ensemble without time-specific information. The GN analog is a prior ensemble of cycle sequences drawn from a stationary generator: a statistical cycle model fitted to the well-observed era, or surface-flux-transport/dynamo simulations. That injects no MM-specific information, which is desirable for variant A. But a Gaussian EnKF update is a poor fit for integer, censored and qualitative sunspot data. Offline EnKF is therefore a better fit for the *proxy* layer of variant B, or as a fast baseline. Full MCMC/NUTS suits the observer layer.
- **(d) Pitfalls.**
  - (i) LMR sets R = PSM regression residual variance. That ignores PSM structural error and understates uncertainty when the PSM is misspecified. The sunspot analog is setting observer noise from overlap-regression residuals alone.
  - (ii) LMR calibrates PSMs on instrumental data, and the GN analog is calibrating isotope PSMs on the modern GN. The proxy-informed GN then cannot be used to validate isotopes (Q5).
  - (iii) Seasonal vs. annual mismatch has a direct analog. 14C in a ring reflects growing-season atmospheric 14C with multi-year carbon-cycle memory, so the observation operator must convolve the daily or monthly GN-driven production with the box-model response. A plain annual mean is not enough.
  - (iv) The ≥25-year-overlap rule in LMR corresponds to a minimum-overlap rule for observer calibration. A BHM can instead admit low-overlap MM observers through pooled priors, but their thresholds will be prior-dominated.
- **(e) Software.** `cfr` and LMR code (Python) can serve as the reference offline EnKF. PRYSM gives the PSM software pattern. For JAX, an EnKF update is easy to vectorize, but the recommended architecture for GN is a NumPyro model whose observer PSMs are vectorized likelihood functions over (observer, day) records, and whose isotope PSMs are differentiable ODE solvers (diffrax), as in ticktack (Q4).
- **(c) Ensemble reporting analog.** Report the GN posterior as `N_draws × N_withheld-realizations` (LMR-style grand ensemble). Here a realization is a random leave-out of observers or proxies, so the spread includes sensitivity to which sources are used, not only within-model posterior uncertainty.

### Gaps
- Hakim et al. (2016), Evans et al. (2013) and Dee et al. (2015) were not re-fetched this session. Details are limited to their well-known PSM decomposition and framework descriptions.
- I did not find an LMR-style DA paper that assimilates integer counts or binary/ordinal observations (non-Gaussian observation operators) in paleoclimate. Gaussian-likelihood EnKF is the norm.
- The latter part of Tardif et al. (2019), after Table 6, was not read. It may contain further discussion of circularity and temporal mismatch.

## 3. Likelihoods for censored, aggregated, interval and qualitative evidence (ordinal documentary indices, absence-of-evidence, "no spots seen in year X" with unknown effort; ecological sighting/extinction and occupancy analogs)

### Takeaway
The best historical-climatology precedent is Camenisch et al. (2022, Clim. Past). It treats Pfister-type ordinal indices with Bayes' theorem. The prior comes from climate-model ensembles mapped to index bins. Historians *elicit* likelihoods p(evidence | index). Crucially, *absence of evidence is data*, more probable under normal than extreme conditions because chroniclers recorded extremes preferentially. For blanket statements with unknown observing days, the closest formal analogs come from ecology: sighting-record extinction inference with uncertain sightings and varying effort (Solow; Kodikara; Clements/Lee/McCarthy), occupancy models with imperfect detection and false positives, and coarse-data theory. In the sunspot literature itself, the Hoyt & Schatten database overinterpreted generic statements, assuming the observer looked every day. Vaquero et al. (2015) addressed this with three interpretation models (a sensitivity analysis). A Bayesian likelihood that marginalizes the unknown observing effort is the natural upgrade.

### Cited Findings
- Pfister-type indices are ordinal scores, typically from −3 (extremely cold) to +3 (extremely warm), with 0 as normal. The conventional "index method" cannot assign values where the record has gaps — [Camenisch et al. 2022, Clim. Past 18, 2449–2462](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/18/2449/2022/); [PDF](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/18/2449/2022/cp-18-2449-2022.pdf)
- Camenisch et al. form the prior from ModE-Sim (40 ECHAM6.3 members from 1420 CE) and CESM-LME (13 members). Each member's seasonal output is sorted into 12 duo-decile quantiles mapped to Pfister indices, so index bins are defined relative to each member's own climatology, not as fixed temperature thresholds — [Camenisch et al. 2022](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/18/2449/2022/)
- Historians assign p(observation | climate) for each index value. The values must sum to 1, each must be ≥0.05, and the highest goes to the originally assigned index — [Camenisch et al. 2022](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/18/2449/2022/)
- On absence of evidence: "the absence of any historical description is more likely for normal seasons than extreme seasons". Observers "were systematically more likely to notice and record extreme conditions than average ones", and "the Bayesian approach can actually turn the absence of observations into useful evidence" — [Camenisch et al. 2022](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/18/2449/2022/)
- Camenisch et al. report the following limitations. Skill cannot be tested for the 15th century (no instrumental overlap). Results depend on historians' subjective likelihoods. Sparse documentation (1450s–60s) weakens inferences. Prior reliability depends on ensemble size. Posterior correlations between the two prior ensembles were 0.78–0.85, versus 0.08–0.25 for the priors, so the data dominate where documentation exists — [Camenisch et al. 2022](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/18/2449/2022/)
- The same framework was applied to winter temperatures in SW China, 1700–1949. It uses a 1–6 Cold Weather Index, two paleoclimate ensembles as priors, and a likelihood accounting for documentary uncertainty (preprint) — [EGUsphere 2025-5326](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2025/egusphere-2025-5326/egusphere-2025-5326.pdf)
- Ming/Qing dynasty records on a 4-level ordinal scale were combined via kriging, quantile mapping to °C, and Bayesian DA with Last Millennium Ensemble simulations — [arXiv 2410.21790](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21790v1)
- The documentary-climatology state of the art (Pfister indices, sources and calibration practice) is reviewed in Brázdil et al. (2005), Climatic Change 70:363–430 — [doi:10.1007/s10584-005-5924-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10584-005-5924-1) [DOI from domain knowledge, not re-fetched]
- Sunspot-specific evidence: in the Hoyt & Schatten (1998) database, a large fraction of MM spotless days are based on general statements of absence over a year or longer. The annual share of spotless days backed by an explicit observational record varies between 10 and 80% — [Vaquero et al. 2015, A&A 577 A71 (PDF)](https://dehesa.unex.es/bitstream/10662/19774/6/201525962.pdf)
- Re-analysis of Gottfried Kirch's correspondence found that several generic statements about spotlessness were "overinterpreted by Hoyt & Schatten (1998) assuming that the observer would have observed each and every day during a long period". It also found errors in dates and group numbers in the database — [Neuhäuser et al. 2018, arXiv 1807.10241](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.10241)
- Vaquero et al. (2015) estimated MM activity from active-day fractions under *three levels of strictness* for interpreting "no spots" entries. They found cyclic peaks even during the MM, with active-day fractions staying low — [A&A 10.1051/0004-6361/201525962](https://www.aanda.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201525962)
- The revised sunspot group collection (Vaquero et al. 2016, Sol. Phys.) eliminated a considerable number of MM observations from Hoyt & Schatten's database. The active-day percentage from the new collection gives a higher activity level than the original data, yet one still compatible with a grand minimum — [doi:10.1007/s11207-016-0982-2](https://www.doi.org/10.1007/S11207-016-0982-2); [arXiv 1609.04882](https://export.arxiv.org/abs/1609.04882)
- Hoyt & Schatten (1998), "Group sunspot numbers: a new solar activity reconstruction", Sol. Phys. 179:189–219 — [doi:10.1023/A:1005007527816](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1005007527816) [DOI from domain knowledge, not re-fetched]
- Ecology analog, extinction from sighting records. Solow (1993) introduced inference of extinction from a sighting record modeled as a Poisson process that stops at extinction (Ecology 74:962–964) — [doi:10.2307/1940821](https://doi.org/10.2307/1940821) [DOI from domain knowledge, not re-fetched]
- Uncertain sightings: Solow et al. (2012) note that earlier methods assumed all sightings valid, or dropped uncertain ones or treated them as certain. Their Bayesian method models valid and invalid sightings as independent Poisson processes, with noninformative priors for the valid-sighting rate and for a measure of uncertain-sighting quality (ivory-billed woodpecker) — [Solow et al. 2012 record](https://findanexpert.unimelb.edu.au/scholarlywork/343018-uncertain-sightings-and-the-extinction-of-the-ivory-billed-woodpecker)
- A Bayesian hierarchical extension with certain and uncertain sightings uses a non-homogeneous Poisson process (Kodikara et al. 2020). The ivory-billed woodpecker most likely went extinct in 1940–1945. With uncertain sightings present, the estimate depends either on the last certain sighting or on where the uncertain-sighting rate drops — [Kodikara et al. preprint](https://saritha.rbind.io/pdf/Non_homogeneous_Poisson_process_MEE_Pre_Print.pdf)
- Search effort: an experimental test of a Bayesian extinction method that explicitly includes search effort found that the method generally performs well. However, it is "susceptible to both changes in search effort through time, as well as how detectable a species is", and the prior probability that the species is extant strongly affects accuracy — [Clements, Lee & McCarthy, PeerJ Preprint 466](https://peerj.com/preprints/466)
- Solow (2005) weights the observation window by cumulative sighting effort when effort is not constant (implemented in the EDE R package) — [EDE vignette](https://cran.nics.utk.edu/cran/web/packages/EDE/vignettes/EDE.html)
- Occupancy modeling: repeated surveys separate detection probability from true presence (MacKenzie et al. 2002, Ecology 83:2248–2255) — [doi:10.1890/0012-9658(2002)083[2248:ESORWD]2.0.CO;2](https://doi.org/10.1890/0012-9658(2002)083[2248:ESORWD]2.0.CO;2). False-positive plus false-negative extension: Royle & Link (2006), Ecology 87:835–841 — [doi:10.1890/0012-9658(2006)87[835:GSOMAF]2.0.CO;2](https://doi.org/10.1890/0012-9658(2006)87[835:GSOMAF]2.0.CO;2) [DOIs from domain knowledge, not re-fetched]
- Presence-only data with unknown effort: Poisson point-process models (Warton & Shepherd 2010, Ann. Appl. Stat. 4:1383) — [doi:10.1214/10-AOAS331](https://doi.org/10.1214/10-AOAS331). Data-integration ("integrated species distribution") models combine presence-only, presence-absence and count data through a shared latent intensity (Isaac et al. 2020, TREE 35:56–67) — [doi:10.1016/j.tree.2019.08.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tree.2019.08.006) [DOIs from domain knowledge, not re-fetched]
- Coarse-data theory: Heitjan & Rubin (1991), "Ignorability and coarse data", Ann. Stat. 19:2244–2253, define *coarsening at random* (CAR). Under CAR, the coarsening mechanism can be ignored in likelihood inference — [doi:10.1214/aos/1176348396](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176348396) [DOI from domain knowledge, not re-fetched]

### Inferences
- **(a) Likelihood for "no spots were seen during year X" with unknown observing days.** Treat the statement as a *coarsened* version of the observer's unrecorded daily record.
  - Let `P0_t(λ_t, θ_i)` be the probability that observer `i` would see zero groups on day `t`. Under a Poisson-thinned group population, `P0_t = exp(−λ_t·π_i)`, where `π_i = P(A > θ_i)`. A NegBin version is used when day-level overdispersion is included.
  - *Bernoulli effort model:* each day is observed independently with probability `q_i` (weather × diligence). The probability that all observed days were spotless is then exactly `L(λ, q_i) = Π_{t∈X} [1 − q_i (1 − P0_t)]`. This form marginalizes over the unknown set of days in closed form. Put a prior on `q_i` (for example, Beta informed by the observer's known habits, letters and climate cloudiness) and sample or integrate it.
  - *Known number of days, unknown dates:* if the statement says "observed n days", `L` is the probability that a random n-subset is all-spotless. A dynamic program over days (`lax.scan`, O(365·n)) gives it exactly in JAX.
  - The Hoyt & Schatten interpretation corresponds to `q_i = 1`, which maximizes the evidential weight of the statement (Neuhäuser et al. 2018's "overinterpretation"). Vaquero et al.'s strict/optimum/loose readings are a discrete sensitivity analysis on `q_i`. The Bayesian version marginalizes over `q_i` instead of choosing one reading.
- **(a) Statement-generation (archive) model and non-ignorable coarsening.** A blanket statement is not CAR if observers were more likely to issue a yearly summary when nothing notable was seen. This mirrors Camenisch's "silence is likelier under normal conditions". It also covers rhetorical or careless statements written even if a small spot was briefly seen.
  - Use a mixture likelihood: `L_stmt = ρ_i·L(λ, q_i) + (1 − ρ_i)·c`. Here `ρ_i` is the probability the statement is literal and `c` is an uninformative constant (an "invalid sighting" component, as in Solow et al. 2012 / Royle & Link 2006).
  - Pool `ρ_i` hierarchically by source type (published treatise, private letter, later compiler's paraphrase).
- **(a) Suspect recorded spotless days.** Use a zero-inflated / false-negative component per observer-epoch: `Y_{i,t} = 0` with probability `ζ_i` regardless of `G_t` (the observer did not really look carefully). This is the occupancy false-negative structure. With minimal MM overlap `ζ_i` is weakly identified, so it needs informative hierarchical priors from well-overlapped eras.
- **(a) Ordinal and qualitative statements** ("many spots", "a large spot", "the sun was clean"). Use an ordered-probit/logit link from the latent `G_t` (or the area of the largest group) to the K categories, with observer- or source-specific cutpoints. Alternatively, follow Camenisch and *elicit* p(statement | G-bin) from historians with a floor (≥0.05) to avoid zero likelihoods. Elicited likelihoods make subjectivity explicit, and they should be varied in sensitivity runs.
- **(a) Active-day-only records** (an observer reports only days with spots; effort unknown). These are presence-only data. Model them as a thinned point process with an observer effort intensity, and integrate them with count data via a shared `λ_t` (the integrated-SDM logic).
- **(c) Change-point analog for MM onset and end.** Solow-type sighting-record inference with effort weighting can be adapted to infer the onset and end dates of the MM regime from "sightings" (active days), with effort-varying rates. The Clements/Lee/McCarthy result (sensitivity to effort changes, detectability and prior) is a direct warning for MM onset dating.
- **(d) Pitfalls.**
  - (i) Assuming `q = 1` (Hoyt & Schatten-style) gives a strongly overconfident low-activity MM.
  - (ii) If `q_i`, `θ_i`, `ζ_i` and `λ_t` are all free for a single isolated MM observer, they are jointly non-identifiable. Only priors and pooling resolve this, so report prior sensitivity.
  - (iii) Elicited likelihoods (Camenisch-style) cannot be skill-tested where no overlap exists. Test them in pseudo-proxy experiments (Q6).
  - (iv) Date errors (Neuhäuser et al.) interact with day-level likelihoods. A misdated active day can contradict a spotless day by another observer, so add a date-offset latent or a robust mixture.
- **(e) Software.** All of these likelihoods are closed form or a small DP, vectorizable in JAX. Discrete latents (`G_t` truncated at, say, ≤20 for the MM, statement validity flags) can be enumerated in NumPyro (`funsor` enumeration) or marginalized by hand. NUTS then handles continuous parameters.

### Gaps
- I did not find a published Bayesian *ordered-probit latent-temperature* model for Pfister indices. Camenisch et al. use discrete Bayes with elicited likelihoods instead, so formal latent-variable treatment of documentary ordinal data appears thin in the literature (or was missed by my searches).
- I found no historical-climatology or astronomy paper that explicitly marginalizes over *unknown observing days* behind a blanket statement. The Bernoulli-effort likelihood above is my synthesis from coarse-data and occupancy ideas, not a published method.
- Full texts of Solow et al. (2012), Kodikara et al. (2020) and Clements et al. were not read. Details come from abstracts and search summaries.

## 4. Prior work combining sunspot numbers and cosmogenic isotopes (and aurorae) probabilistically

### Takeaway
The isotope-to-solar-activity chain is well established but mostly *deterministic with Monte Carlo error propagation*. Usoskin et al. (2021) run the chain 14C production → geomagnetic correction → open solar flux → sunspot number "outside of grand minima", with Monte Carlo uncertainties. Wu et al. (2018) did a χ²/Monte Carlo search for the modulation potential from multiple 10Be series plus 14C. Fully Bayesian joint inversions are recent. Schanner, Nilsson & Muscheler (2025, Sol. Phys.) jointly infer solar modulation and the geomagnetic field from 14C/10Be production rates with GP priors, a bimodal (grand-minimum) prior, and NumPyro NUTS on JAX. ticktack (JAX + diffrax) supplies a differentiable carbon box model. I found no Bayesian model that jointly infers GN itself from sunspot observers + 14C + 10Be + aurorae; variant B would be novel.

### Cited Findings
- Usoskin et al. (2021), "Solar cyclic activity over the last millennium reconstructed from annual 14C data", A&A 649, A141 (arXiv 2103.15112). It starts from the 14C production rate from the most precise tree-ring measurements and proceeds in three physics-based steps: "(1) Correction of the 14C production rate for the changing geomagnetic field; (2) Computation of the open solar magnetic flux; and (3) Conversion into sunspot numbers outside of grand minima". Uncertainties are handled by Monte Carlo — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.15112.pdf); [author PDF](https://cc.oulu.fi/~usoskin/personal/aa40711-21.pdf); [A&A highlight](https://www.aanda.org/2021-highlights/2188)
- Reconstructed cycles were validated against sunspot records from 1750 onward and generally agree. A conference abstract reports 85 cycles starting in 971 CE (provisional figure) — [SpaceClimate8 abstract](https://spaceclimate8.uws.edu.pl/seeabstract.php?id=19); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.15112.pdf)
- A corrigendum to Usoskin et al. (2021) was published in A&A 664, C3 (2022); its content was not checked — [corrigendum PDF](https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/pdf/2022/08/aa40711e-21.pdf)
- The annual 14C record (Brehm et al. 2021, "Eleven-year solar cycles over the last millennium revealed by radiocarbon in tree rings", Nat. Geosci. 14:10–15) underlies Usoskin et al. (2021) — [doi:10.1038/s41561-020-00674-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41561-020-00674-0) [DOI from domain knowledge, not re-fetched]
- Wu et al. (2018, A&A 615 A93), "Solar activity over nine millennia: a consistent multi-proxy reconstruction", combined six 10Be series with the global 14C production series. They used a Monte Carlo χ² search for the most probable modulation potential and reported 6–7-millennia variability with lows near 5500 BC and 1500 AD — [arXiv 1804.01302](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/1804.01302)
- An earlier 14C-based sunspot reconstruction used physics-based models with parameters fixed by independent measurements. It concluded that the recent ~70-yr high-activity episode was the most intense and longest in 8,000 years (Solanki et al. 2004, Nature 431:1084) — [author PDF](https://cc.oulu.fi/~usoskin/personal/NAture_NW.pdf)
- The open solar flux (OSF) from sunspot-number continuity model, the physical link used in step (2), is Solanki, Schüssler & Fligge (2000), Nature 408:445 — [doi:10.1038/35044027](https://doi.org/10.1038/35044027) [DOI from domain knowledge, not re-fetched]
- Schanner, Nilsson & Muscheler (2025), Sol. Phys. 300(11):150, "Joint inversion of radionuclide production rate data and thermoremanent magnetic records over the Holocene" — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12592250/); [doi:10.1007/s11207-025-02559-0](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s11207-025-02559-0):
  - Solar modulation φ (force-field) uses a GP on 22-yr knots (Matérn, τ = 25.6 yr), mapped through an inverse CDF that is a weighted mixture of two beta CDFs. The mixing weight κ controls bimodality. Optional long-term GP and 11-yr quasi-periodic kernels are included, the latter only with annual 14C over the last 3,000 years.
  - The geomagnetic field uses Gauss coefficients to degree 5 on 50-yr knots, tied to Kalmag at 1900/1950/2000.
  - The forward model gives production rates as quadratic-surface approximations of published production models in (dipole moment, φ), with hemispheric 10Be asymmetry.
  - 10Be comes from GRIP and EDML ice cores (22-yr bins). 14C comes from IntCal20 Δ14C converted to production through a carbon-cycle model (referenced, not detailed), plus annual Brehm et al. data for the 11-yr test.
  - Noise is 5% for 14C and 10% for 10Be production, with per-class scale factors ~N(1, 0.05). Geomagnetic observations use a Student-t (4 d.o.f.) likelihood.
  - Inference uses PyMC with NumPyro NUTS on JAX (4 chains, 1,000 warmup). Some posteriors (about 40 thermoremanent ages) had R-hat problems and were excluded.
  - Regime: no explicit switching. A low-activity mode near 375 MV and a regular mode near 665 MV emerge, and the data push κ upward.
  - Sunspot numbers are not used for calibration or validation (the gluing uses neutron-monitor φ). The 11-yr cycle result is a proof of concept with poor S/N in places. The 10Be scaling factors appear to absorb dipole/quadrupole tension.
- A predecessor (Nilsson et al. 2022, Earth Planets Space) tested a joint solar–geomagnetic model on synthetic data with realistic noise. It then reconstructed solar activity and the dipole from 14C production over two millennia, in agreement with independent geomagnetic models and with activity inferred from the group sunspot number — [search summary; Lund profile](https://www.geologi.lu.se/andreas-nilsson/publication/b4c48b88-6916-487e-a46f-7cb2bdd5a1b9); [GFZ record](https://gfzpublic.gfz.de/pubman/item/item_5036897) (full text not read)
- ticktack is the first open-source Python package linking carbon-cycle box models with modern Bayesian inference. It is built on JAX, solves the box-model ODEs with diffrax, and connects to emcee MCMC. It was used to infer parameters of the six known Miyake events from all public annual tree-ring 14C data — [Zhang et al., arXiv 2210.13775 (Proc. R. Soc. A)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.13775); [JOSS 10.21105/joss.05084](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.05084.pdf)
- The MM was reassessed with multiple datasets as "indeed a grand minimum" (Usoskin et al. 2015, A&A 581 A95) — [doi:10.1051/0004-6361/201526652](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201526652) [DOI from domain knowledge, not re-fetched]
- Aurorae: Stangl & Foelsche (2024) argue that aurora-based reconstructions before the 1800s face serious difficulties, especially around the MM. The main databases conflict, and a Schröder catalogue covering the whole MM contradicts the common compilations — [Atmosphere 2024 (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/fab0996c84174f9fa4d9058e8c8f0402). Older catalogues (e.g., Krivsky & Pejml, latitude <55°) show very few aurorae before the MM — [IAU review of grand-minima records](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/0C4E944B8DDB6AF2688323248E485703/S1743921312005121a.pdf/historical-records-of-solar-grand-minima-a-review.pdf)
- A 2026 arXiv preprint, "Reconstruction of pretelescopic and early telescopic solar activity cycles from auroral records", exists; only its reference list was visible to me — [arXiv 2609.14143](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.14143)

### Inferences
- **(a) Forward model chain for variant B.** Daily `G_t` → (rotational/annual aggregation) → OSF via the Solanki–Schüssler–Fligge continuity ODE (emergence ∝ GN, decay timescale) → heliospheric modulation φ (empirical OSF–φ relation) → `Q14(φ, M)`, `Q10(φ, M)` → archive models (ticktack carbon box ODE for Δ14C; deposition/transport plus accumulation for 10Be) → measurements. Every link has parameters with priors, and links are differentiable (diffrax), so the full chain can be sampled with NUTS. Schanner et al. already run the φ/geomagnetic part in NumPyro, so their production surrogates and priors can be reused.
- **(a) Aurora data model.** Treat yearly or event aurora records at site latitude `L` as a thinned point process. Rate `= r(OSF or φ, L) × effort_site(t)`, with heavy catalog-level discrepancy terms and source-reliability mixtures (Q3), given the catalog conflicts.
- **(c) Grand-minimum prior.** Schanner's mixture-of-betas inverse-CDF prior is a template for a GN process prior with an explicit low-activity mode. In variant A, its hyperparameters must not be fitted to isotope records (circularity). Options are a weakly-informative mixture weight, or a prior from dynamo-model ensembles (the LMR/Camenisch "model ensemble as prior" approach).
- **(d) Pitfalls.**
  - (i) Usoskin et al.'s step (3) uses an OSF→SN conversion calibrated on the sunspot era and explicitly not applied inside grand minima. That calibration is a nonlinear, regime-dependent forward link. Variant B must let it be uncertain or regime-dependent, or it will be overconfident in the MM.
  - (ii) Schanner's tight scale-factor prior (N(1, 0.05)) and 5–10% noise could understate structural error from carbon-cycle and transport model choices. Add model-discrepancy terms (e.g., a GP discrepancy in the Kennedy & O'Hagan 2001 sense, JRSS-B, [doi:10.1111/1467-9868.00294](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9868.00294) [DOI from domain knowledge, not re-fetched]) or Student-t likelihoods.
  - (iii) Temporal mismatch: daily GN versus annual 14C (carbon-cycle smoothing attenuates the 11-yr amplitude) versus 22-yr-binned 10Be versus event-level aurorae. Each needs an explicit aggregation or convolution operator, as in Steiger et al. 2014 and Li et al. 2010.
  - (iv) Calibration drift: geomagnetic dipole uncertainty and 10Be climate/deposition effects change the proxy-to-φ mapping over time. Treat these mapping parameters as slowly varying, not fixed.
  - (v) Calibrating isotope PSMs on the modern GN (post-1850) hits the anthropogenic Suess effect and post-1950 bomb 14C. That is known domain context; no source was fetched for it here. The calibration window for 14C–solar links is therefore pre-industrial and overlaps the GN era where GN itself is uncertain, so calibration must be joint, not sequential.
- **(e) Software.** Use NumPyro + JAX for the observer layer, diffrax for the OSF and carbon-box ODEs (ticktack code is directly reusable), and Schanner's PyMC→NumPyro production surrogates as a reference.

### Gaps
- I found no published Bayesian joint model of *GN* (observer-level sunspot data) with 14C, 10Be and aurorae. Schanner et al. (2025) infer φ, not GN, and use no sunspot data. Usoskin et al. (2021) are deterministic chain plus Monte Carlo.
- Usoskin et al. (2021) full text was blocked (A&A returned 403). The exact OSF→SN calibration, Monte Carlo ensemble size and corrigendum changes are unverified.
- No probabilistic forward model for aurora occurrence versus solar activity was found in this session. The 2026 arXiv preprint's methods were not visible.
- Lockwood/Owens-type OSF continuity-model updates and their uncertainty were not searched here; other researchers may cover them.

## 5. How the paleoclimate community avoids circularity when a reconstruction is later used to validate proxies

### Takeaway
Four practices transfer to GN. Keep calibration and verification data disjoint, as in LMR's 25% withheld proxies per Monte Carlo realization and verification against an independent instrumental product. Use a time-invariant (climatological) prior that injects no period-specific information. Run multi-method ensembles (PAGES 2k 2019). Run perfect-knowledge pseudo-proxy tests. For GN, the two-variant design is itself the main firewall. It holds only if variant A's priors and hyperparameters are also free of isotope information, and if variant B is never used to "validate" the isotopes that went into it.

### Cited Findings
- In LMR, instrumental-era verification uses the same period as PSM calibration, which the authors acknowledge. They mitigate this with calibration–validation experiments (Supplement S3) and by using Berkeley Earth, which was not used for calibration, as the main spatial reference. Proxy-space verification uses the withheld 25% of records — [Tardif et al. 2019](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/15/1251/2019/)
- The LMR prior (CCSM4 LM anomalies) contains no time-specific information (no volcanic events or 20th-century trend), so any time-specific signal in the posterior comes from proxies — [Tardif et al. 2019](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/15/1251/2019/)
- Camenisch et al. note that their 15th-century reconstruction cannot be skill-tested because there is no independent overlap. This illustrates the limit when only the target evidence exists — [Camenisch et al. 2022](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/18/2449/2022/)
- PAGES 2k Consortium (2019, Nat. Geosci. 12:643–649) reconstructed global mean temperature with multiple statistical methods (including a BHM and offline DA) from one proxy database. Agreement across methods was a robustness check — [doi:10.1038/s41561-019-0400-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41561-019-0400-0) [DOI from domain knowledge, not re-fetched]
- Pseudoproxy experiments let method errors be separated from data errors because the truth is known. Smerdon cautions that translating PPE results to real-world implications must be done carefully — [Smerdon 2012, WIREs Clim. Change 3:63–77](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/wirecc/v3y2012i1p63-77.html); [Smerdon blog summary](https://www.ldeo.columbia.edu/~jsmerdon/Site/Research_Blog/Entries/2012/1/22_A_Pseudoproxy_Review.html)
- In the isotope–sunspot literature, Usoskin et al. (2021) *validate* their 14C-based cycles against sunspot records after 1750, while relying on a sunspot-calibrated OSF→SN conversion — [arXiv 2103.15112](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.15112.pdf)
- Schanner et al. (2025) avoid sunspot data entirely (gluing on neutron-monitor φ). That makes their φ an independent target for testing a sunspot-only GN — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12592250/)
- Pareto-smoothed importance-sampling leave-one-out (PSIS-LOO) gives efficient approximate leave-one-out predictive checks from a single posterior fit (Vehtari, Gelman & Gabry 2017, Stat. Comput. 27:1413) — [doi:10.1007/s11222-016-9696-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11222-016-9696-4) [DOI from domain knowledge, not re-fetched]

### Inferences
- **(c) Firewall rules for the two-variant GN design.**
  1. *Variant A purity.* No isotope or aurora information may enter data, priors or hyperpriors. Concretely, the grand-minimum regime prior, cycle-amplitude priors and the MM-era threshold population prior must come from sunspot-era data or physics models (dynamo/SFT), and never from 14C/10Be-informed reconstructions such as Usoskin et al. 2021 or Wu et al. 2018. Document this as a "data lineage" table.
  2. *Variant B usage.* Variant B can be used for downstream solar-forcing studies but not to test 14C/10Be/aurora proxies. Also run leave-one-proxy-type-out versions (B−14C, B−10Be, B−aurora). Each can then be scored against the withheld proxy through posterior-predictive checks in proxy space (the LMR withheld-proxy logic).
  3. *Leave-one-observer-out within variant A.* Use PSIS-LOO or explicit K-fold by observer to score prediction of withheld observers' daily counts and blanket statements. This is the sunspot analog of withheld proxies.
  4. *Independent targets.* Isotope-only posteriors that use no sunspots (e.g., Schanner-type φ) are the right comparison for variant A. Isotope reconstructions calibrated on SN (e.g., Usoskin 2021 step 3) are partially circular as validation targets for any GN.
  5. *Multi-method ensemble.* Compare the Bayesian GN against ADF, backbone and other existing GN series as a PAGES-2k-style method spread. This is not validation, but a check on structural uncertainty.
- **(d) Hidden circularity sources to flag in the methods paper.** (i) Observer selection or exclusion criteria tuned by agreement with isotope-based MM levels. (ii) Choosing among Vaquero's strict/loose interpretations because one "matches 14C". (iii) Using an MM-specific prior "known" from isotopes.
- **(d) Weak-identifiability warning.** Where the data are uninformative (MM years with only blanket statements), variant A's posterior equals its prior. Comparisons with isotopes in those years then test the *prior*, not the sunspot evidence. Report per-year prior-to-posterior variance contraction so readers can see where tests are meaningful.

### Gaps
- The PAGES 2k (2019) and Vehtari et al. (2017) papers were not re-fetched. Their roles are summarized from well-known content.
- I did not find a paleoclimate paper that formally runs "leave-one-proxy-type-out, then validate the reconstruction against the withheld type" as a named protocol. LMR's random 25% withholding is the closest documented practice.

## 6. Pseudo-proxy experiments (PPEs) as the benchmarking paradigm, and an RGO-based synthetic-observer design for the GN methods paper

### Takeaway
PPEs are the accepted way to benchmark reconstruction methods. Take a known "truth" (in paleoclimate, a millennial GCM run), degrade it with realistic sampling and noise into pseudo-proxies, reconstruct, and score against the truth. Li et al. (2010) validated their BHM this way, and BARCAST has been tested on synthetic long-memory fields. For GN, the Royal Greenwich Observatory (RGO; plus Debrecen) area-resolved group catalogue plays the role of the GCM truth. Synthetic observers are made by applying thresholds, observing-day masks transplanted from real historical observers, and archive/compiler processes. Smerdon's caution applies: PPE skill does not transfer if the synthetic degradation is easier than reality, and RGO contains no grand minimum.

### Cited Findings
- Millennial-length climate simulations serve as controlled test beds for evaluating reconstruction methods via PPEs. Any real-world reconstruction is the product of multiple uncontrolled factors, and PPEs isolate them. The translation of PPE results into real-world implications "must be done cautiously" — [Smerdon 2012 (IDEAS/RePEc record)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/wirecc/v3y2012i1p63-77.html); [Smerdon blog: pseudoproxy review](https://www.ldeo.columbia.edu/~jsmerdon/Site/Research_Blog/Entries/2012/1/22_A_Pseudoproxy_Review.html); [Making pseudoproxies](https://www.ldeo.columbia.edu/~jsmerdon/Site/Research_Blog/Entries/2011/3/11_Making_pseudoproxies.html)
- In a pseudoproxy comparison of CFR methods over North America and Europe, no individual method produced CFRs with universally superior spatial error statistics. Hemispheric means were insufficient to evaluate spatial skill — [Smerdon et al. 2011 GRL](https://www.ldeo.columbia.edu/~jsmerdon/papers/2011_grl_smerdonetal.pdf)
- In PPEs, surrogate pseudoproxy and pseudo-instrumental data are fed to reconstruction techniques, and the result is compared with the simulation's true target. BARCAST has been tested on synthetic long-memory target fields — [Nilsen, Werner & Divine 2018](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/14/947/2018/cp-14-947-2018.pdf)
- Li, Nychka & Ammann (2010) validated their multiproxy BHM in a simulation experiment with a GCM run as truth and synthetic tree-ring, pollen and borehole proxies, plus forcings — [UCAR record](https://data.ucar.edu/sq/dataset/the-value-of-multiproxy-reconstruction-of-past-climate)
- BARCAST Part II scored reconstructions by the coefficient of efficiency in surrogate scenarios against RegEM — [Tingley & Huybers 2010b](https://phuybers.sites.fas.harvard.edu/Doc/Tingley_JC2010b.pdf)
- Nilsson et al. (2022) tested their joint solar–geomagnetic Bayesian model on synthetic data with realistic noise before applying it to real 14C — [GFZ record](https://gfzpublic.gfz.de/pubman/item/item_5036897) (per search summary)
- A 2023 Scientific Data paper produced a pseudoproxy emulation of the PAGES 2k database using PSMs, and it is linked from the `cfr` package (a reusable PPE infrastructure precedent) — [PyPI cfr](https://pypi.org/project/cfr/2024.8.1)

### Inferences
- **(a) GN PPE design (RGO-based synthetic truth).**
  1. *Truth.* RGO/Debrecen daily group lists with areas give the true `G_t` and the area distribution per day. The repo's recent commits mention a calibrated RGO–Debrecen daily-area CSV. For variant B tests, push the true GN through the isotope forward chain (OSF → φ → ticktack Δ14C, plus a 10Be model) to make pseudo-isotopes.
  2. *Synthetic observers (sensor).* Draw thresholds `θ_i` from a realistic population, including slow drifts (random walk or step changes for instrument changes), and apply them to the true area lists.
  3. *Observing-day masks.* Transplant real historical observers' calendars (e.g., actual MM-era observing-day patterns) onto RGO cycles. Minimal overlap is then reproduced realistically, not idealized.
  4. *Archive/compiler degradation.* Convert some synthetic observers' records into yearly blanket statements with a known statement-generation model (literal vs. rhetorical; informative coarsening). Inject false spotless days, date offsets (Julian/Gregorian-like shifts) and transcription errors at rates suggested by Neuhäuser et al. 2018 / Vaquero et al. 2016.
  5. *Low-activity truth.* RGO has no grand minimum. Construct MM-like truths by thinning RGO groups (scaling `λ_t` down by 10–100×), or by using dynamo/SFT simulations with grand minima. Smerdon's caution means skill on scaled RGO may not transfer to a real regime change.
  6. *Misspecification PPEs.* Generate with one forward model and fit with another: a different threshold link, a different effort model, a different carbon-box model or production yield. These tests measure *overconfidence*, which is the main failure mode the methods paper must quantify.
- **(a) Scoring.** Use RMSE/CE on annual and monthly GN; CRPS; central-interval coverage of the posterior ensemble (calibration of uncertainty); PIT histograms; skill specifically in low-activity windows; recovery of observer thresholds and drift; and, for variant B, posterior-predictive skill on withheld pseudo-isotopes.
- **(c) Comparison set.** Run the Bayesian model, ADF and other GN methods on identical pseudo-observer sets (the PAGES 2k multi-method idea). Report where each fails, as Smerdon et al. 2011 showed that no single method is universally best.
- **(d) Pitfalls.** (i) A "perfect-model" PPE, where synthetic data are generated by the same model used for inference, overstates skill. Nilsen et al. note earlier BARCAST tests generated targets from BARCAST's own AR(1) equations. (ii) Synthetic observers drawn independently ignore correlated errors (e.g., observers copying each other or shared compilers), so include them. (iii) The RGO era's cycle statistics differ from 17th-century conditions. (iv) Scoring only annual means can hide failures in daily detection modelling, which matters because the MM is evaluated mainly through active-day fractions.
- **(e) Software and scale.** The PPE generator is cheap: numpy/JAX vectorized thinning of RGO area lists. Each reconstruction run is the costly part. Plan hundreds of PPE realizations using SVI or short NUTS runs on reduced-resolution latents, with full NUTS only for a representative subset. JAX `vmap` across PPE realizations on GPU is feasible if the model has fixed shapes, so pad observer records.

### Gaps
