# Seven fields trade daisy chains for latent counts

Seven fields outside heliophysics have faced the same structure as the group-number (GN) problem: many imperfect, drifting observers of one latent quantity, linked by sparse overlaps. All of them dropped pairwise chaining for a single joint generative model. In that model a latent true state is seen through observer-specific forward models, all overlaps are fitted at once, a few references set the scale, and the result is published as an ensemble of realizations. Photometric surveys, satellite climate records, test equating, whole-history chess ratings, paleoclimate hierarchical models and ecological N-mixture models arrived at this design independently. The Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) shows what chaining costs: its chained calibration's errors were dominated by the weakest link, and the global fit removed the striping the chain left behind ([Padmanabhan et al. 2008](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/astro-ph/0703454)).

For GN, the highest-payoff transfer is a concurrent hierarchical model with three parts. Its observation layer thins the RGO/Debrecen group-area distribution with a soft, drifting, observer-specific detection function. Its target is the count a reference observer would have made, which is the only absolute scale unmarked counts can identify ([Barker et al. 2018](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70198028)). Its latent daily group population is an immigration-death chain, marginalized exactly by a forward filter in JAX and sampled backward to give the posterior GN ensemble; the researchers estimate about 5×10⁸ flops per likelihood evaluation. Three identifiability traps recur in every field and should frame the methods paper. Absolute scale cannot be recovered from counts alone. Drift shared by an entire era looks exactly like real solar change. Chained links accumulate random error and add systematic error coherently.

Current practice is weakest on zero days and blanket statements. ADF simulates observers by removing days at random ([Usoskin et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1512.06421)), and the Hoyt & Schatten database read generic statements as if observers had looked every day ([Neuhäuser et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.10241)). Ecology, epidemiology and historical climatology supply missing-data likelihoods that the researchers adapted to both problems. Simulation-based inference (SBI) is best used to calibrate how observers report, not to estimate GN itself. No published Bayesian model infers GN jointly from observers plus ¹⁴C, ¹⁰Be and aurorae, so a proxy-informed variant would be new, but only if a documented data-lineage firewall keeps the sunspot-only variant free of isotope information.

## Five layers, seven donor fields, three recurring traps

The GN problem splits into five layers, and each adjacent field has a mature solution for one or two of them. The **activity layer** is a slowly varying emergence rate λ(t), with an ~11-year cycle and grand minima. The **population layer** is the daily number of groups on the visible disk, N_t, set by emergence, area-dependent lifetimes and rotation. The **detection layer** is each observer's probability of reporting a group of a given area, plus splitting/merging conventions and career drift. The **reporting layer** covers which days were observed and written down: weather, diligence, under-reported spotless days and yearly blanket statements. The **anchor layer** is the RGO/Debrecen catalogue of group identities and areas, optionally joined by cosmogenic isotopes and aurorae.

| Donor field | Core modeling idea | GN layer it supplies | Signature identifiability lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ecology | Latent abundance thinned by detection that depends on observer and size; integrated likelihoods across data types | Population, detection, data integration | Unmarked counts identify only relative abundance without auxiliary detection data |
| Counting processes | Immigration-death dynamics (INAR, M/G/∞) with binomial or mark-dependent thinning; selection models for missing data | Population, reporting | Under-reporting is identified only through assumed dynamics, priors or validation data |
| Crowdsourcing, psychometrics, ratings | Latent truth with a confusion matrix per rater; concurrent calibration; whole-history smoothing | Detection, global linking, drift | Era-wide drift ("rating inflation") is invisible to relative comparisons |
| Instrument and survey intercalibration | One global self-calibration of all zero-points from all overlaps; ensembles over structural choices; blind benchmarks | Global linking, uncertainty | Exact null modes and slow "tilt" modes need anchors or priors; the sampling pattern biases trends |
| Paleoclimate and historical evidence | Process/data/parameter hierarchies; proxy system models; Bayesian documentary evidence; pseudo-proxy tests | Reporting (blanket statements), anchors (isotopes), validation | Sparse eras become prior-dominated; reconstructions used to validate their own inputs are circular |
| Simulation-based inference | Neural posteriors or likelihoods trained once on a simulator and reused; coverage diagnostics | Detection (emission calibration) | Misspecification and overconfidence, worst outside the training distribution |
| Scalable JAX/NumPyro inference | Exact marginalization of small discrete states by forward filtering; parallel scans; blocked samplers | All layers jointly | The binding limits are sequential latency and gradient cost, not memory |

Three traps run through all seven fields.

**Absolute scale.** N-mixture theory finds that without marked individuals or auxiliary detection data, reliable inference is mainly for relative abundance ([Barker et al. 2018](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70198028)). SDSS found that "the worst constrained mode is, as expected, the zero point of the calibration which is exactly degenerate" ([Padmanabhan et al. 2008](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/astro-ph/0703454)).

**Common-mode drift.** Under multiplicative thinning, the likelihood does not change if every active observer's detection is multiplied by c(t) and λ(t) is divided by c(t) *(derived)*. The same symmetry makes chess "inflation" a quantity set by priors. It also explains why satellite matchups cannot fix absolute calibration coefficients when instruments are collinear ([Zou et al. 2006](https://pubs.ssec.wisc.edu/research_Resources/publications/pdfs/ITSC15/zou01_ITSC15_2006.pdf)).

**Chain error.** Two research streams independently derived the same result. In a weighted least-squares link graph, the variance of an observer's scale relative to the reference equals the effective resistance between the two nodes. Along a single chain that variance grows linearly with the number of links, and parallel paths reduce it. Systematic per-link bias, by contrast, adds coherently and never averages down *(derived)*. The sections below read each domain against these three traps.

## Ecology already wrote the GN likelihood as an N-mixture model

### Latent counts, observer thinning and the formal version of ADF

The static N-mixture model treats site abundance as N_i ~ Poisson(λ_i) and each replicate count as y_ij | N_i ~ Binomial(N_i, p_ij), marginalized by summing over N up to a finite bound ([Royle 2004](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/n-mixture-models-estimating-population-size-spatially-replicated-counts); [Kéry & Royle, AHM ch. 6](https://www.mbr-pwrc.usgs.gov/workshops/ahm2023/06_N-mixture/Chap.%206%20AHM1%202016.pdf)). Dail & Madsen made it dynamic. Survivors are binomial with probability ω, recruits are Poisson(γ), and the population need be closed only within a primary period ([Dail & Madsen 2011](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1541-0420.2010.01465.x) [nv]). Hostetler & Chandler explain why ecologists prefer this class to Gaussian state-space models: Gaussian models allow negative abundance, treat false positives and false negatives as equally likely, and contain parameters that cannot be estimated. Their extension adds trend, Ricker and Gompertz growth, zero-inflation and random effects, with code ([Hostetler & Chandler 2015](https://repository.si.edu/items/ce4eaaee-084c-4548-8beb-51d53304441c/full)).

**Researchers' derivation.** In GN terms, the site is the Sun on day t. The visits are the observers reporting that day, ω is daily group survival and γ_t is emergence following the cycle. The population is almost exactly closed within a day. Because there is only one Sun, replication has to come from autocorrelated days rather than independent sites. That pushes the design toward dynamic rather than static models.

The tightest link to current practice comes from occupancy models. In the Royle-Nichols model, detection heterogeneity comes from abundance: the probability of detecting at least one individual is p_i = 1 − (1 − r)^{N_i} ([Royle & Nichols 2003](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/5224229)). The researchers show that an observer's active-day fraction, as a function of activity, is exactly such a curve *(derived)*. With area-dependent detection it becomes 1 − Π_g(1 − F_o(A_g)), and it is identified once the reference supplies the distribution of counts and areas at each activity level. The same reading explains ADF's known strengths and weaknesses *(derived)*. The presence/absence channel ignores splitting conventions, but it saturates at high activity, when nearly every day is active.

Occupancy work on false positives adds two pieces. Royle & Link's model needs the constraint p11 > p10 to avoid label switching ([Royle & Link 2006](https://doi.org/10.1890/0012-9658%282006%2987%5B835:GSOMAF%5D2.0.CO;2) [nv]). Miller et al.'s "multiple detection methods" design, in which one method is assumed to give no false positives, beat both earlier approaches in simulation ([Miller et al. 2011](https://www.originalwisdom.com/wp-content/uploads/bsk-pdf-manager/2019/03/Miller-et-al_2011_improving-occupancy-estimation-when-two-types-of-observational-error-occur.pdf)). On overlap days RGO/Debrecen plays that near-certain method *(derived)*.

Two more ecological ideas bear on the reporting layer. When survey times depend on the latent state, estimates are biased unless the sampling is modeled jointly with the state ([Conn, Thorson & Johnson 2017](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/52909)). eBird's "complete checklist" flag, carried by almost 90% of checklists, is what licenses treating unreported species as zeros ([Goldstein & de Valpine 2022](https://link.springer.com/10.1038/s41598-022-16368-z)). The researchers propose an analogous completeness flag for historical sunspot records *(derived)*.

### Size-biased detection gives GN an advantage ecology lacks

Distance sampling is the template for area-threshold detection. Small clusters are harder to see, so the observed mean cluster size is biased upward. The standard remedy is to make detection a function of size, but size-as-covariate fits can fail to converge when sizes span several orders of magnitude ([Distance practical 9](https://distancesampling.org/online-course/09-clusters/R-prac/Pr9-instructions.html); [Distance size-bias remedy](https://distancesampling.org/Distance/articles/web-only/groupsize/Remedy-size-bias-for-dolphin-surveys.html)). That warning applies directly to sunspot areas, which range from a few to thousands of millionths of a hemisphere. Observers in double-observer surveys also differ in effective detection radius, the direct analog of observer-specific thresholds ([Nichols et al. 2000](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/5223914)).

**Researchers' derivation.** GN has an advantage distance sampling never gets: the reference catalogue supplies the full area distribution of undetected groups. Bin area so that N_{t,b} ~ Poisson(λ_t π_b), with π_b from RGO/Debrecen. Poisson thinning then makes one observer's count Poisson with mean λ_t Σ_b π_b F_o(A_b), with no latent sum. On reference days the group list and areas are known, so the observer's count is exactly Poisson-binomial with probabilities F_o(A_g). That is distance sampling with known distances, which is strongly identified. Selection runs in the same direction as size bias, so historically detected groups were larger than average. Any per-group statistic inferred from historical data, such as hemispheric asymmetry or latitude, inherits that selection unless it is reweighted.

### Observer random effects, novices and aging come from bird surveys

The North American Breeding Bird Survey (BBS) model is a log-linear Poisson model. It has stratum intercepts, trends and year effects, an observer-route random effect, a first-year (novice) indicator and an overdispersion term, and it is fitted by MCMC ([Sauer & Link 2011](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70036906); equation details [nv]). Every variant of the current implementation keeps the observer, route and first-year terms ([bbsBayes2](https://bbsbayes.github.io/bbsBayes2/articles/models.html)). The empirical record behind these terms is strong. Observer differences affected counts for about half of 369 species ([Sauer, Peterjohn & Link 1994](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/5222733)). Removing each observer's first year lowered average trend estimates for **415 of 459 species (90%)** ([Kendall, Peterjohn & Sauer 1996](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/5223223)). Older observers recorded fewer detections for 13 of 43 species in the Ontario atlas, and detections fell with observer age for 59 of 64 BBS species, most strongly for high-pitched calls ([Farmer et al. 2014](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4203298/)).

**Researchers' derivation.** The BBS observer effect is a hierarchical Bayesian k-factor, and its multiplicative form is wrong for thresholds. At low activity a larger share of groups is small, so the observer-to-reference ratio depends on activity. The random-effect machinery should be kept, but moved onto the threshold and slope of a detection function, with novice offsets and a career random walk. The Kendall result also warns about the secular trend: uncorrected early-career undercounting in long single-observer stretches would turn directly into a spurious GN trend. Where only one observer is active, as in parts of the Maunder Minimum, observer effects cannot be separated from year effects. Only the hierarchical prior and smoothness priors constrain them there.

Citizen-science work estimates skill without pairwise overlap. Species-accumulation curves index observer skill and steepen with continued participation ([Kelling et al. 2015](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4599805/)). An effort-adjusted expertise score raised reporting rates for **197 of 200 species** ([Johnston et al. 2018](https://research-portal.st-andrews.ac.uk/en/publications/estimates-of-observer-expertise-improve-species-distributions-fro/)). Expertise can also be modeled as a latent class ([Yu, Wong & Hutchinson 2010](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/5694101/authors)). The researchers read the ADF "fingerprint" as the same device and point out its circularity *(derived)*. A fingerprint has to condition on an activity estimate that comes from other observers. Embedding it in one joint likelihood avoids the two-stage loop, which is the main argument against running ADF as a separate pre-calibration step.

### Integrated models join counts, the catalogue, statements and proxies

Integrated population models (IPMs) multiply independent data likelihoods that share latent states and parameters ([Besbeas et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.0006-341X.2002.00540.x) [nv]). In simulations, violating independence had minimal effect, and joint analysis improved precision and accuracy, especially with small samples ([Abadi et al. 2010](https://boris.unibe.ch/5197)). What matters is probabilistic independence, which does not follow automatically from the data coming from different physical individuals ([Barraquand 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01877)).

**Researchers' derivation.** For GN, four components share the latent daily population: daily observer counts; the RGO/Debrecen catalogue, playing the "demographic" role that mark-recapture data play in ecology; blanket statements, entered as censored observations; and isotope or auroral proxies, entered as index components. Three pitfalls follow. An observer's daily counts and that observer's own published means must not both be entered. Transcribed duplicates in compilations must be removed. Shared weather calls for a day-by-region random effect. The large RGO component will dominate shared parameters, so each component needs its own posterior predictive checks and its own era-specific parameters.

### Identifiability: define the target relative to the reference

The 2018 robustness literature is blunt. With visit-varying detection the model is overspecified. Even with constant detection, alternative models in which abundance is non-identifiable produce practically indistinguishable data ([Barker et al. 2018](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70198028)). Small amounts of double counting, unmodeled change in abundance, or unmodeled change in detection produce large biases that goodness-of-fit tests often miss ([Link et al. 2018](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70198662)). Across 137 bird data sets from 2,037 sites, Poisson and zero-inflated Poisson N-mixtures had almost no estimability problems, while negative-binomial mixtures had problems in about a quarter ([Kéry 2018](https://doi.org/10.1002/ecy.2093)). The notes reconcile the two camps: Kéry measures numerical estimability, while Link and Barker measure bias under misspecification.

**Researchers' derivation.** One observer's marginal counts identify only the product λp. For two observers on the same day, λ = E[y₁]E[y₂]/Cov(y₁, y₂). But all observers miss the same small groups. That shared heterogeneity inflates the covariance and biases λ, which is exactly the Link fragility mechanism. Observer splitting is the "double counting" violation. The defensible strategy is to anchor detection to the reference catalogue and define the target as the reference-equivalent count, a relative-abundance target of the kind Barker et al. regard as robust.

### Scalability and software

Marginalizing discrete latent states made Bayesian population models **5 to more than 1000 times faster**, with close parameter inferences and latent states recoverable afterwards ([Yackulic et al. 2020](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70209184)). A normal-approximation likelihood handles large counts and doubles as an estimability diagnostic ([Madsen & Royle 2023](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70274657)). Beta-binomial (correlated) detection was often among the best-supported models across 396 eBird data sets ([Goldstein & de Valpine 2022](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9296480/)). Mature tools are available for cross-checks: unmarked's `pcountOpen`, `occuRN`, `occuFP` and `distsamp` ([Kellner et al. 2023](https://cran.ms.unimelb.edu.au/web/packages/unmarked/citation.html)); [nmixgof](https://jknape.r-universe.dev/nmixgof/doc/manual.html); [spAbundance](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.19446v2); and [nimbleEcology](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/nimbleEcology/citation.html). Because GN counts are small, exact marginalization is cheap *(derived)*. The researchers found no application of N-mixture models to sunspot data. They also found no quantitative guidance on how many simultaneous observers identifiability needs when detection is heterogeneous.

## Queueing theory recasts ADF as a busy-period estimator

### Integer autoregressions give lifetimes, and a sharp warning

Binomial thinning, α∘X = Σ_{i=1}^{X} Y_i with Bernoulli(α) terms, defines the INAR(1) recursion: the thinned previous count plus new arrivals. Its moments are E[α∘X] = αμ_X and Var = α²σ²_X + α(1 − α)μ_X ([Weiß 2008](https://dx.doi.org/10.1007/s10182-008-0072-3), as summarized in search results; [Scotto, Weiß & Gouveia 2015](https://statmod.org/smij/Vol15/Iss6/Scotto/Abstract.html)). Hidden INAR models add a second thinning to estimate the reporting probabilities of under-reported diseases ([Fernández-Fontelo et al. 2016](https://www.stat.ubc.ca/sites/default/files/abstract-22jun2017.pdf)), and later versions let that probability vary with season or trend ([Fernández-Fontelo et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.00262)). Bracher's critique is the key result for GN. A Poisson INAR(1) with time-homogeneous under-reporting is equivalent to a fully observed INAR(∞) with geometric lags, so "estimated reporting probabilities depend on the assumed lag structure of the latent process" ([Bracher 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06688)). The original authors' reply was not read.

**Researchers' derivation.** In GN terms, α is the daily probability that a visible group is still visible, combining intrinsic decay with rotation off the west limb. The innovations are emergences plus groups rotating onto the east limb, and visible residence times are geometric with mean 1/(1 − α). One observer's autocorrelation is ρ_Y(k) = pα^k, so p is identified only by the drop between lag 0 and lag 1. Sunspots break that channel twice. Detecting a given group persists from day to day rather than being independent, and lifetimes are heavy-tailed rather than geometric. **An observer's detection level must come from overlap with the reference or with chained observers, never from the observer's own dynamics.** The dispersion index after thinning, D_Y = 1 + p(D_N − 1), shrinks toward 1 as acuity falls. That gives a weak, model-dependent extra handle on p when D_N is known from RGO.

### M/G/∞ inference is "emergence rate and lifetime from daily counts"

A mature nonparametric literature estimates an M/G/∞ queue's arrival rate λ and service-time distribution G from the number of busy servers alone ([Bingham & Pitts 1999](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1003831118254)). Goldenshluger gives the central identities. Occupancy is marginally Poisson with mean λ/μ, the covariance is R(t) = ρH(t), and therefore **1 − G(t) = −R′(t)/λ**. The estimator attains the optimal but slow rate T^{−β/(2β+2)}. The same work distinguishes three observation schemes: arrival and departure epochs, queue length on a grid, and the **busy-period indicator 1(X(t) > 0)** ([Goldenshluger 2016](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1508.00076)). Variants handle data in which only busy-period durations are seen, by deconvolution ([Hall & Park 2004](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jorssb/v66y2004i4p861-875.html)), and nonhomogeneous arrivals ([Goldenshluger & Koops 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1805.10353)).

**Researchers' derivation.** ADF uses only 1{Y_{o,t} > 0}, which is scheme (iii). Under Poisson occupancy, P(Y = 0) = exp(−Λ_o(t)), so the active-day fraction is a monotone transform of the detected mean. Cycle variation and emergence clustering break that simple inversion. Hall & Park's deconvolution implies that the run lengths of spotless and active days near minimum carry information on lifetimes and acuity that GN work rarely uses.

Rotation enters through a visibility kernel. Campbell's theorem then predicts that the daily GN autocovariance decays quickly over about 13.5 days, dips near zero, and shows a recurrence bump near 27 days for long-lived groups. Poisson colouring gives an exact, closed-form pairwise likelihood. Groups seen on both of two days, only the first, or only the second form independent Poisson classes. Any two days, or any two observers on one day, are therefore bivariate Poisson, at cost O(T × lags). Because nonparametric recovery of G is slow, **G and the rotation kernel should be learned from RGO/Debrecen and held fixed for historical observers.**

### Mark-dependent thinning explains why k-factors depend on activity

Thinning a Poisson or Cox process independently keeps it Poisson or Cox, with intensity multiplied by the detection fraction. Astronomy's MATHPOP models observed globular clusters as a thinning of the true marked process that depends on magnitude and location ([MATHPOP](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.06040)). Gaussian Cox models treat the thinned points as latent variables ([arXiv 2203.06743](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.06743)). Current ADF sits at the hard-threshold limit, in which an observer "would miss all of the groups with an area smaller than S_S" ([Usoskin et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1512.06421)). The 1749–1996 update holds that threshold constant over each career ([Usoskin et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.05109)). ADF is most precise at moderate activity ([Willamo, Usoskin & Kovaltsov 2018](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1803.10501)).

**Researchers' derivation.** A soft logistic acuity, π_o(a) = logistic((log a − log S_o)/w_o), nests the hard threshold as w_o → 0. It gives Hamiltonian Monte Carlo smooth gradients and takes a hierarchical random walk on log S_o for drift. The expectation of detection over marks can be precomputed as a differentiable lookup table, by Monte Carlo over empirical RGO/Debrecen group tracks on a grid of (S_o, w_o). This avoids augmenting latent points entirely.

The effective detection fraction q_o = E_F[π_o(a)] depends on the area distribution F. When F shifts with activity, the observer-to-reference ratio becomes non-linear, which is consistent with ADF's activity dependence. The critical assumption is that F, or F given activity, is stationary across centuries. Counts cannot test this, and the methods paper must say so plainly.

### Epidemiology's under-reporting models transfer almost directly

The Pogit-type hierarchy has a latent Poisson truth, binomial reporting and covariates on both layers. Stoner, Economou & Drummond handle fully under-reported data with an informative prior on the mean reporting rate. They validate it with simulation-based prior and covariate sensitivity analyses plus prior and posterior predictive checks ([Stoner et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.00544)). Dvorzak & Wagner add spike-and-slab selection for both layers ([pogit](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/pogit/html/pogit-package.html)).

Nowcasting tools contribute three transferable pieces: reporting parameters that follow random walks with change points ([surveillance::nowcast](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/surveillance/html/nowcast.html); [NobBS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7162546)), partial pooling ([epinowcast](https://epinowcast.r-universe.dev/epinowcast/doc/manual.html)), and joint estimation of the epidemic curve with the reporting process. Their delay machinery does not transfer.

Poisson Approximate Likelihoods (PAL) "require only elementary arithmetic operations and no tuning parameters" and are consistent in the large-population limit ([Whitehouse, Whiteley & Rimella 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13602)). A 2025 extension integrates out time-varying reporting ([LawPAL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.06935v2)). GN counts of 0–30 are far from the large-population regime, so PAL must be validated against the exact forward algorithm before adoption *(derived)*.

### Spotless days are a selection problem, not zero inflation

Zero-inflated Poisson models add structural zeros ([Lambert 1992](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/technometrics/90-00/vol-34-01/v3401001.pdf)). Missing-not-at-random data can be factored two ways, as selection models or as pattern-mixture models ([arXiv 2108.13286](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.13286)). Both rest on sensitivity parameters the data cannot identify ([VicBiostat](https://www.vicbiostat.org.au/node/2107)), and mappings between the two forms exist for Poisson outcomes ([Kaciroti & Raghunathan 2014](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/bitstream/2027.42/109600/1/sim6302.pdf)). ADF simulates observers by removing days at random ([Usoskin et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1512.06421)), which is an MCAR (missing completely at random) assumption.

**Researchers' derivation.** The GN record is zero-*deflated*: spotless days are selectively missing. The selection model logit P(R_{o,t} = 1 | Y) = γ_o + s(day of year) + δ_o·1{Y = 0} enters the forward recursion in closed form. The parametric form identifies δ_o only weakly. Three channels do better: same-day overlap with other observers or the reference; a recording rate that falls at solar minimum, which weather cannot explain; and record gaps that cluster inside spotless runs. Results should be reported on a grid of δ values, a tipping-point analysis. A hurdle model is exactly ADF's binary channel.

### Replicate counts without identities only bound the detectable population

Multiple-systems estimation without group identities reduces to N-mixture models ([Royle 2004](https://cals.arizona.edu/classes/wfsc578/Royle%202004.%20N-mixture%20models%20for%20estimating%20population%20size.pdf)). Their estimates can diverge to infinity when detection and the number of occasions are small ([Dennis, Morgan & Ridout 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4406156)). Chao's heterogeneity estimator, n + f₁²/(2f₂), is a lower bound ([Chao 1987](https://gwern.net/doc/statistics/order/capture/1987-chao.pdf)).

**Researchers' derivation.** Area-dependent acuity is shared heterogeneity, so same-day counts estimate only the groups the best observers could detect. Hence the recommendation to define the target as N_t^ref. The bound N_t ≥ max_o Y_{o,t} holds only for observers who do not over-split, so it works best as a diagnostic that flags splitters. Where drawings with positions exist, as for Staudacher, Schwabe and many 19th-century observers, groups can be matched across observers. That makes true capture-recapture possible, and it is the strongest auxiliary detection data GN could obtain.

## Rater and equating models show why daisy chains drift

### Dawid-Skene generalizes the probability-distribution-matrix calibration

The Dawid-Skene model gives each annotator a confusion matrix P(reported label | true class) and estimates those matrices together with the latent true classes. NumPyro ships a Bayesian version built on Paun et al.'s comparison of annotation models ([NumPyro annotation example](https://num.pyro.ai/en/0.9.2/examples/annotation.html); [Paun et al. 2018](https://preview.aclanthology.org/moar-dois/Q18-1040.pdf); [Raykar et al. 2010](https://jmlr.org/papers/v11/raykar10a.html)).

The sunspot baseline it generalizes is Chatzistergos et al.'s non-parametric calibration of **314 observers**. Each observer gets a probability-distribution matrix built from overlap days with a backbone observer, errors are propagated by Monte Carlo, and acuity thresholds are assumed constant over each career ([Chatzistergos et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.06183)). Sunspot-network work supports a near-Poisson count model. A log-log regression of conditional variance on conditional mean gives a slope of about **0.96** for group counts, against about 1.5 for spot counts, and zeros are best handled by a hurdle model rather than zero inflation ([Mathieu et al., arXiv 2009.09810](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2009.09810)). Model-based aggregation beats majority vote mainly when items have three or fewer annotations or workers are unreliable ([arXiv 1902.08918](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.08918), secondary).

**Researchers' derivation.** The probability-distribution matrix is a Dawid-Skene confusion matrix estimated pairwise against a stand-in for truth, the backbone observer. Its conditioning direction should be checked in Chatzistergos et al.'s Section 2. The latent-truth version uses every observer on a day, propagates the backbone's own noise, and gives a posterior on days the backbone never saw.

GN needs four changes to the classic model. The global class prevalence gives way to a dynamic prior on G_t driven by a latent log λ_t. Confusion matrices become physically structured, built from threshold thinning plus a split/merge kernel, because a free 30×30 matrix per observer amounts to about 900 unidentifiable parameters. A reporting model that depends on the true class handles under-reported zeros. Random-walk drift handles careers that change. The Li et al. result predicts that most of the gains will land in the sparse early centuries, which are also the hardest to validate.

### The hierarchical rater model separates daily truth from activity

The Hierarchical Rater Model (HRM) treats each rating as an indicator of an "ideal" category, which in turn indicates proficiency. It was built because facets models appear to give infinitely precise proficiency as raters are added, since they ignore the dependence among ratings of the same response ([Patz et al. 2002](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/~brian/trs/patz-et-al-hrm-jebs-2002.pdf)). DeCarlo et al. put a latent-class signal-detection model at the rater level ([DeCarlo, Kim & Johnson 2011](https://www.tc.columbia.edu/faculty/ld208/faculty-profile/files/JEM_11.pdf)).

Uto's Bayesian many-facet Rasch model gives each rater's severity a random walk, β_rt ~ N(β_{r,t−1}, σ_r), with a lognormal prior on σ_r. The Markov prior improved RMSE in every simulated condition, by **0.079, 0.089 and 0.124**, and the gain grew as designs became sparser. Prior location mattered: μ_σ = −5 over-shrank real change, and μ_σ = 0 was unstable. The paper does not discuss separating rater drift from true change ([Uto 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10615980/)). Raters in a speaking assessment became more lenient as a group over time, an example of common-mode drift ([Neittaanmäki & Lamprianou 2024](https://jyx.jyu.fi/bitstreams/6a3cd46c-1776-4f26-9beb-9268f7b78535/download), secondary).

**Researchers' derivation.** The HRM's levels map directly onto GN: proficiency is λ_t, the ideal category is G_t, and ratings are observer reports. The "infinite precision" warning becomes a design decision. Many observers can pin down G_t, but not λ_t, because G_t scatters around λ_t in a way every observer shares. The paper must therefore state which of the two it reports. Rating studies separate drift from true change because essays have fixed quality and are assigned to dates at random. A GN day happens only once, so drift shared by a whole era is confounded with solar change.

### Equating quantifies chain drift and offers a graph-wide fix

The non-equivalent-groups-with-anchor-test (NEAT) design of test equating has two main methods: chained equating and post-stratification. They rest on different missing-data assumptions, neither of which can be tested ([ETS 2008](https://www.ets.org/research/policy_research_reports/publications/article/2008/gpll.html); [Sinharay & Holland 2009](https://www.ets.org/research/policy_research_reports/publications/report/2009/hvss.html), secondary). Kernel equating, implemented in `kequate`, gives standard errors for the difference between two equating functions ([kequate](https://cran.r-universe.dev/kequate/doc/manual.html)).

Three secondary results describe long chains. Single-link conversions drift further from operational ones as equatings accumulate, and Guo et al. model that drift as a random walk ([Guo et al. 2011](https://www.ets.org/research/policy_research_reports/publications/report/2011/jdyg.html)). Only systematic error induces scale drift ([Haberman & Dorans](https://www.ets.org/research/policy_research_reports/publications/report/2011/imyj.html)). Linking to two or three old forms, instead of one, stabilized conversions (Liu, Guo & Dorans 2014, as summarized in [CASMA RR 54](https://education.uiowa.edu/sites/education.uiowa.edu/files/2022-10/casma-research-report-54.pdf)).

Simultaneous "multiple linking" solves all constants on the link graph at once, with standard errors ([equateMultiple, Haberman 2009 / Battauz 2017](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/equateMultiple/refman/equateMultiple.html)). Battauz has also developed tests of whether different chains give different results ([Battauz 2022](https://air.uniud.it/retrieve/f290da09-07d4-4033-8235-48704184e713/Statistica%20Neerlandica%20-%202022%20-%20Battauz%20-%20Testing%20for%20differences%20in%20chain%20equating.pdf), title-level). Concurrent calibration, which fits everything in one model, is more accurate when the model fits. Separate calibration was more accurate when there were few common items ([NWEA](https://www.nwea.org/uploads/2021/06/Robust-IRT-Scaling_NWEA_conferencePaper.pdf); [ACT RR 99-08](https://act.org/content/dam/act/unsecured/documents/ACT_RR99-08.pdf), secondary).

**Researchers' derivation.** The correspondence is exact:

| GN method | Equating analog |
|---|---|
| Backbone calibration | Chained equating |
| Matrix or regression calibration on overlap days, applied to a whole career | Linking that assumes population invariance |
| ADF | Equipercentile equating with groups assumed equivalent at each activity level |
| A joint latent model | Concurrent calibration |
| Graph shortest-path linking | Single-path linking, which multiple linking generalizes to all paths |

Willamo et al.'s test of activity dependence ([arXiv 1803.10501](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.10501)) is GN's population-invariance check, and every method should run it. Two independent parallel paths halve link variance, which matches the multiple-old-form result. Systematic bias shared across paths does not average down. The equating evidence also gives the practical design. Build the concurrent Bayesian model as the primary product. Keep a Haberman-style linking of separately estimated pairwise calibrations as a diagnostic. Treat disagreement between the two as a misspecification signal.

### Whole-history ratings show what an absolute scale costs

Glicko treats time-varying paired-comparison strengths as a state-space model ([Glickman 1999](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jorssc/v48y1999i3p377-394.html)). Whole-History Rating finds the jointly most probable rating history and predicts better than Elo, Glicko and TrueSkill ([Coulom 2008](https://www.remi-coulom.fr/WHR/)). TrueSkill Through Time (TTT) smooths each player's yearly skill forward and backward, and reported that overall playing strength rose over 150 years ([Dangauthier et al. 2007](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/3331-trueskill-through-time-revisiting-the-history-of-chess)). Its 2025 implementation "propagates all historical information throughout a single causal network" and handles millions of nodes on low-end hardware ([Landfried & Mocskos 2025](https://www.jstatsoft.org/article/view/v112i06)). Chessmetrics sidesteps inflation by defining ratings as dominance over contemporaries ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparison_of_top_chess_players_throughout_history), secondary).

**Researchers' derivation.** If Y_{o,t} ~ Poisson(k_o(t)λ_t), then conditional on a same-day total n, Y_A ~ Binomial(n, k_A/(k_A + k_B)). That is a Bradley-Terry likelihood with λ_t eliminated, so overlap days are literally "match results". In a random-walk model, posterior variance grows linearly with time since the last informative comparison. The scale uncertainty of the 17th and 18th centuries is therefore the random-walk variance accumulated along career handoffs, a temporal version of effective resistance. Forward-only filters such as Elo depend on processing order, and so does a backbone run from the present backward. Smoothers use every link in both directions. The likelihood depends only on differences in log strength, so TTT's 150-year rise is driven by the slow-drift prior. GN cannot adopt Chessmetrics' relative scale, because an absolute count is the scientific target. Features of the count distribution that a pure rescaling cannot change, such as the fraction of single-spot groups, could break the symmetry.

Astronomy citizen science already uses these models. Space Warps gave each volunteer a 2×2 confusion matrix trained on simulated lenses, with a prior lens probability of 2×10⁻⁴ ([Marshall et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.06148)). DynIBCC tracked classifiers whose performance changed over time, reporting variational Bayes as faster than Gibbs sampling, 13 s against 349 s ([Simpson et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.1831v1), secondary).

## Survey calibration proves global fits beat chains and topology sets the floor

### Ubercal: simultaneous solution, unconstrained modes and the drift tilt

SDSS ubercalibration "simultaneously solves for the calibration parameters and relative stellar fluxes using overlapping observations". It reached **about 1% relative error over 8,500 deg² in griz and about 2% in u** ([Padmanabhan et al. 2008](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0703454)). The earlier calibration was a chain: BD+17°4708 to the USNO network to the 20-inch Photometric Telescope (PT) to secondary patches. Its errors were "dominated by the Photometric Telescope (PT) based calibration method". Against DR4, ubercal differed by about 2% rms, with striping traced to the chain's patches. Adding long cross-scans, the "Apache Wheel", to connect weakly linked regions cut their errors from **10.4 to 7.8 mmag**.

Several further SDSS results matter for GN. The zero-point was exactly degenerate, and weakly constrained eigenmodes were projected out and restored with Gaussian priors. A time derivative of extinction that was held fixed rather than fitted accumulated into a **~10 mmag survey-wide tilt**, which the authors call the most serious systematic. Noise-free simulations reached about 0.1%, so the residual error came from model misspecification rather than from statistics ([Padmanabhan et al. 2008](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/astro-ph/0703454)).

Holmes, Hogg & Rix compared four survey strategies of equal size. A regular grid aligned in all passes never converged and gave 1.17% source rms, while a quasi-random strategy gave 0.13%. Their advice is "Do not use a regular, repeated tiling of the sky", because connectivity matters more than uniform exposure ([Holmes, Hogg & Rix 2012](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1203.6255)). Pan-STARRS found that the reference survey, SDSS, contributed about as much to their mutual differences as Pan-STARRS itself ([Schlafly et al. 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.2208)). The Dark Energy Survey's forward model (FGCM) showed that sub-percent calibration needs a chromatic term, the passband convolved with the source spectrum ([Burke et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.01542)).

**Researchers' derivation.** In log space each overlap is y_ij = a_i − a_j + ε_ij. The weighted least-squares covariance is then the pseudo-inverse of the graph Laplacian, so Var(a_i − a_j) equals the effective resistance R_ij. Along a pure chain, variance grows linearly with the number of links. Parallel paths combine like parallel resistors, so a global solution is never worse than the best single chain. Systematic per-link bias, however, grows linearly in N rather than as √N; this is the SDSS tilt in GN form. The Laplacian has one exact null mode per connected component, and its smallest non-zero modes concentrate at bottlenecks. For GN those bottlenecks are the 17th to early 18th century and the gaps around the late 18th century. The methods paper should report the effective resistance from every observer to the reference and the number of connected components in each era. The backbone corresponds to the PT chain, and observers with long careers act like the Apache Wheel. A scalar k-factor is FGCM's gray approximation; a detection function applied to an area distribution that changes with activity is the chromatic correction. RGO/Debrecen should be modeled as an observer with its own uncertainty, not as truth.

### Satellite records teach ensembles, collinearity and structural spread

NOAA STAR recalibrated the microwave sounders from raw counts using simultaneous nadir overpass matchups, within 100 s and 111 km, with a linear-plus-small-quadratic calibration. "SNO matchups alone cannot determine the absolute values of the coefficients because there is a high degree of colinearity" ([Zou et al. 2006](https://pubs.ssec.wisc.edu/research_Resources/publications/pdfs/ITSC15/zou01_ITSC15_2006.pdf), via summary). RSS produced **400 error realizations** per dataset, each of which can be added to the measurements to give one plausible version of the observations. On long time scales, inter-satellite merging matters at all levels ([Mears et al. 2011](https://mural.maynoothuniversity.ie/6529); [Mears ITSC-17](https://itwg.ssec.wisc.edu/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/6.13_mears_itsc17.pdf)).

For SBUV merged ozone, merging uncertainty dominates trends since 2001. Trends that look significant on statistical error alone become non-significant at 2σ once instrument uncertainty is included ([Frith et al. 2017](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/17/14695/2017/)). A single adjustment component can move a trend a long way: RSS V4 raised the lower-troposphere trend from 0.136 to 0.184 K/decade ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAH_satellite_temperature_dataset), secondary, not checked against the primary paper). Double-differencing compares two sensors that never observe simultaneously through a transfer standard, either a third sensor or a radiative-transfer model ([NOAA report](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/65872)). The GSICS satellite-intercalibration program reports bias at a standard scene radiance because bias depends on the scene ([Hewison](https://ipwg12.imgw.pl/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/S10.9_Hewison_GSICS_updated.pdf)).

**Researchers' derivation.** Same-day observations are GN's simultaneous overpasses. A latent G(t) in a Bayesian model is the radiative-transfer transfer standard, so exact overlaps become unnecessary wherever the latent-process prior is credible across gaps. Scene-dependent bias corresponds to a k-factor that depends on activity. Collinearity predicts the drift-versus-trend confound: if many 19th-century observers upgraded telescopes over the same decades, their shared drift aliases into the secular trend. Only stable reference observers, priors on G(t) or external proxies can break it. The RSS practice transfers as published posterior realizations. Structural ensemble members should differ in drift parameterization, choice of reference (RGO, Debrecen or a modern observer), observation model and observer-inclusion rules. Trend uncertainty should be decomposed by component, as Mears et al. do.

### TSI composites show uncertainty depends on time scale

Dudok de Wit et al.'s total solar irradiance (TSI) composite uses a multiscale maximum-likelihood estimator with data-driven uncertainties and handles gaps naturally ([Dudok de Wit et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.02341)). Instrument precision, taken from AR(5) innovations, ranges from about 0.07 W/m² (TIM) to 0.8 W/m² (ERBE), and the residuals have near-**1/f** spectra. An instrument that is not observing keeps a weight of σ⁻² multiplied by exp(−2|T_k|/a), where T_k is the distance to its nearest observation and a is the time scale; this is how the ACRIM gap is bridged. Uncertainty comes from Monte Carlo over more than 1,000 composites. Only the 1996–2009 decrease is significant at 5%, whereas under white noise all three trends would appear significant. Degradation correction was deliberately kept outside the merge ([full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1702.02341)). A Gaussian-process fusion concludes that the trend in these composites is mostly an artifact of coloured noise ([Montillet et al. 2022](https://essopenarchive.org/users/560943/articles/608699-data-fusion-of-total-solar-irradiance-composite-time-series-using-41-years-of-satellite-measurements), secondary).

**Researchers' derivation.** In a latent Gaussian-process or state-space model, the exponential gap weight emerges automatically. An observer can be good at one time scale and bad at another, for example tracking cycle phase well but biasing amplitude. Residual spectra between each observer and the reference should be estimated in overlaps before a drift prior is chosen. If they are 1/f, secular GN uncertainty grows much faster with time scale than a white-noise analysis predicts. Unlike the TSI composite, GN should model drift inside the likelihood. That means accepting the drift-versus-trend degeneracy openly rather than hiding it in upstream corrections.

### Sea level shows the sampling pattern itself biases trends

Hay et al.'s Kalman smoother gave a 1901–1990 global-mean rate of **1.2 ± 0.2 mm/yr**, against 1.6–1.9 in earlier analyses. Binning by region with the real gauge sampling and missing data reproduced 1.6 ± 0.4 from the sampling pattern alone. Bootstrap estimates levelled off about 0.4 mm/yr above the smoother: "combined spatial sparsity and missing data generate an upward bias" ([Hay et al. 2015 summary](https://sealeveldocs.readthedocs.io/en/latest/hay15.html); numbers from that documentation page, not the Nature PDF). Dangendorf et al. independently obtained 1.1 ± 0.3 mm/yr. They attribute the lower rate roughly equally to vertical-land-motion correction, area weighting and a common datum ([Dangendorf et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5468634)).

**Researchers' derivation.** The GN diagnostic is to impose the real historical observer-day calendar and selection on a known truth, run the competing methods, and measure the induced trend and amplitude biases. Dangendorf's three corrections map onto observer drift, weighting by representativeness rather than by record count, and a consistent absolute anchor. The researchers note that the probabilistic and earlier sea-level reconstructions differed by about 30%. A comparable gap between a full-Bayes GN and the backbone or ADF series in poorly sampled centuries is plausible but untested, and the paper should be prepared to attribute any such difference to specific sampling or bridging effects.

### Station homogenization supplies benchmarks and break attribution

NOAA's Pairwise Homogenization Algorithm (PHA) tests difference series against neighbours for undocumented shifts and attributes each shift to the responsible station ([Menne & Williams 2009](https://ncei.noaa.gov/pub/data/ushcn/papers/menne-williams2009.pdf); full text not parsed). A randomized, perturbed ensemble of PHA was benchmarked on synthetic analogs. For Tmin, the ensemble error bounds include the unhomogenized trends ([Williams, Menne & Thorne 2012](https://mural.maynoothuniversity.ie/6495)). Berkeley Earth cuts records into fragments at breakpoints and recombines them by kriging ([Rohde & Hausfather 2020](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/12/3469/2020/)). Bayesian changepoint methods quantify uncertainty in shift position and size ([Hannart & Naveau](https://www.lsce.ipsl.fr/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/HannartNaveauTechnometrics.pdf)), and priors that favour documented events raise detection power ([Li, Lund & Hewaarachchi](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1511.07238)). At low but realistic signal-to-noise, **even a random segmentation explains about half the break variance** ([Lindau & Venema 2018](https://ascmo.copernicus.org/articles/4/1/2018/)).

The HOME blind intercomparison received **25 blind contributions plus 22 after the inserted inhomogeneities were revealed**. It scored centred RMSE at several averaging scales, trend error and break-detection skill. The best performers were designed to work with an inhomogeneous reference, and automatic methods matched manual ones ([Venema et al. 2012](https://opac.geologie.ac.at/ais312/dokumente/copernicus.org/cp-8-89-2012.pdf)). Synchronous breaks across stations defeated ACMANTv4 in the MULTITEST benchmark ([Domonkos 2023](https://www.mdpi.com/2225-1154/11/11/224)), and a daily-resolution benchmark now exists ([Killick 2016](https://ore.exeter.ac.uk/repository/handle/10871/23095)).

**Researchers' derivation.** A career change is a station break: a new telescope, a new counting convention, failing eyesight, or an assistant taking over. PHA's attribution logic transfers, but it is weak before about 1850 and impossible for observers with only one overlap partner. Cutting observers into pseudo-observers at breaks replaces an assumed drift form with extra nuisance parameters. Every cut, however, raises effective resistance and lets real change leak away.

The benchmark blueprint for the methods paper starts from RGO/Debrecen truth with group areas and lifetimes. On it go synthetic observers with thresholds, seeing noise, counting conventions, drift and breaks, some documented and some not. These observers are placed on the real historical calendar and overlap graph, including selective reporting of zeros, and every method runs blind. Scoring uses HOME metrics extended to cycle amplitudes, secular trends and **coverage of posterior intervals**, and stress tests add synchronous breaks. As Holmes et al. did, the truth generator must differ from the fitted model.

## Paleoclimate turns blanket statements and isotopes into likelihoods

### Hierarchical reconstructions and proxy system models

BARCAST specifies "simple parametric forms for the spatial covariance and temporal evolution of the climate field, along with observation equations linking data types to the true field". It outperforms RegEM when its assumptions "are not strongly violated" ([Tingley & Huybers 2010b](https://phuybers.sites.fas.harvard.edu/Doc/Tingley_JC2010b.pdf); [2010a](https://phuybers.sites.fas.harvard.edu/Doc/Tingley_JC2010a.pdf), structure from domain knowledge). Proxies with uncertain dates can be sampled jointly inside such a reconstruction ([Werner & Tingley 2015](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/11/533/2015/cp-11-533-2015.pdf)). Li, Nychka & Ammann combined proxies of different temporal resolution with forcings, and validated the model against a GCM run used as truth ([UCAR record](https://data.ucar.edu/sq/dataset/the-value-of-multiproxy-reconstruction-of-past-climate)). Statistical reviews argue for forward data models over inverse regression ([Tingley et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.quascirev.2012.01.012) [nv]; [Wikle & Berliner 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physd.2006.09.017) [nv]).

The Last Millennium Reanalysis (LMR) is the operational precedent ([Tardif et al. 2019](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/15/1251/2019/)). It uses an offline ensemble square-root Kalman filter. Its prior is drawn from a climate simulation and contains "no time-specific information". Each proxy has a linear proxy system model (PSM) whose error variance is set to the regression residual, and proxies need at least 25 years of calibration overlap. Each of 51 realizations withholds 25% of the proxies, giving **100 members × 51 realizations, a 5,100-member grand ensemble**. Proxy system modeling splits each forward model into sensor, archive and observation submodels ([Evans et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.quascirev.2013.05.024) [nv]; [PRYSM](https://doi.org/10.1002/2015MS000447) [nv]). Time averages enter through the observation operator ([Steiger et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1175/JCLI-D-12-00693.1) [nv]). The Python package `cfr` bundles PSM calibration, LMR and validation ([cfr](https://pypi.org/project/cfr/2024.4.30)).

**Researchers' derivation.** Each historical observer gets a three-part PSM. The sensor model turns aperture, seeing and acuity into p_i(A) = Φ((log A − log θ_i(t))/s_i). The archive model describes the medium (drawings, tallies, letters or yearly statements), so a blanket statement is coarsening at the archive level. The observation model covers transcription and dating errors introduced by compilers. Separating the three distinguishes "saw less" from "wrote less" from "mis-entered", which threshold-only calibration conflates. Monthly and annual means become aggregation operators on the daily latent. Julian-Gregorian and transcription errors become latent date offsets on individual records.

A stationary process prior fitted to cycles after 1750 can pull Maunder Minimum activity toward normal cycles, so the paper should report per-year contraction of the posterior relative to the prior. A Gaussian ensemble Kalman update fits integer, censored and qualitative sunspot data poorly. It belongs in the proxy layer or a fast baseline. LMR's withheld realizations translate into an ensemble of posterior draws × random leave-outs of observers.

### Absence of evidence is data, if effort is modeled

Camenisch et al. treat ordinal Pfister-type documentary indices with Bayes' theorem. The priors come from climate-model ensembles: ModE-Sim's 40 members and CESM-LME's 13. Historians elicit the likelihoods, each with a floor of 0.05. Because "the absence of any historical description is more likely for normal seasons than extreme seasons", "the Bayesian approach can actually turn the absence of observations into useful evidence". Posterior correlations between the two ensembles reached **0.78–0.85, against 0.08–0.25 for the priors**, so the data dominate where documentation exists. The 15th century cannot be skill-tested ([Camenisch et al. 2022](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/18/2449/2022/)).

The sunspot record has exactly this problem. In the Hoyt & Schatten database, the annual share of spotless days backed by an explicit observational record varies between **10 and 80%** ([Vaquero et al. 2015](https://dehesa.unex.es/bitstream/10662/19774/6/201525962.pdf)). Generic statements in Kirch's correspondence were "overinterpreted by Hoyt & Schatten (1998) assuming that the observer would have observed each and every day" ([Neuhäuser et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.10241)). Vaquero et al. read "no spots" entries at three levels of strictness and still found cyclic peaks during the Maunder Minimum ([A&A 577 A71](https://www.aanda.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201525962)). The revised collection removed many Maunder Minimum observations and gave a higher activity level, still compatible with a grand minimum ([Vaquero et al. 2016](https://export.arxiv.org/abs/1609.04882)).

Ecology's extinction-from-sightings models supply the effort machinery. Uncertain sightings are modeled as a separate Poisson process ([Solow et al. 2012](https://findanexpert.unimelb.edu.au/scholarlywork/343018-uncertain-sightings-and-the-extinction-of-the-ivory-billed-woodpecker)), later extended to non-homogeneous processes ([Kodikara et al. 2020](https://saritha.rbind.io/pdf/Non_homogeneous_Poisson_process_MEE_Pre_Print.pdf)), and the observation window is weighted by search effort ([EDE](https://cran.nics.utk.edu/cran/web/packages/EDE/vignettes/EDE.html)). A field test found these methods susceptible to changes in effort, to detectability and to the prior ([Clements, Lee & McCarthy](https://peerj.com/preprints/466)).

**Researchers' derivation, the main new likelihood in the notes.** Treat "no spots were seen during year X" as a coarsened version of the observer's unrecorded daily record. Under Poisson thinning, the chance of a spotless report on day t is P0_t = exp(−λ_t π_i), with π_i = P(A > θ_i). Suppose each day was observed independently with probability q_i (weather times diligence). The statement's likelihood is then exactly **L = Π_{t∈X}[1 − q_i(1 − P0_t)]**. This marginalizes over the unknown set of observed days in closed form, with a prior on q_i.

Existing readings are special cases. Hoyt & Schatten's reading sets q_i = 1, which maximizes the statement's weight. Vaquero's three readings are a discrete sensitivity analysis on q_i, whereas the Bayesian version integrates over q_i.

Four extensions follow. If the statement says "observed n days" without dates, a dynamic program of cost O(365·n) gives the likelihood exactly. Statements may have been written precisely because nothing notable was seen, so the coarsening is not ignorable; a mixture ρ_i L + (1 − ρ_i)c, pooled by source type (treatise, letter, later paraphrase), absorbs rhetorical statements. Ordinal phrases such as "many spots" or "the sun was clean" take an ordered-logit link or Camenisch-style elicited likelihoods. Records that list only spotted days are presence-only data. For a single isolated Maunder Minimum observer, q, θ, the false-zero rate and λ are jointly non-identifiable, so prior sensitivity has to be reported.

### Isotopes and aurorae: a deterministic chain awaiting a joint model

Usoskin et al. convert annual ¹⁴C to sunspot numbers in three physics-based steps: a geomagnetic correction, open solar flux, then sunspot numbers "outside of grand minima". Uncertainties come from Monte Carlo, and the result is validated against sunspots after 1750 ([Usoskin et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.15112.pdf); corrigendum [A&A 664 C3](https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/pdf/2022/08/aa40711e-21.pdf), content not checked). Wu et al. combined six ¹⁰Be series with ¹⁴C through a Monte Carlo χ² search ([Wu et al. 2018](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/1804.01302)).

Schanner, Nilsson & Muscheler run the first fully Bayesian joint inversion of solar modulation and the geomagnetic field. They use a GP on 22-year knots mapped through a mixture-of-betas inverse CDF that permits bimodality. Production noise is 5% for ¹⁴C and 10% for ¹⁰Be. Inference uses **NumPyro NUTS on JAX**. The posterior shows a low-activity mode near 375 MV and a regular mode near 665 MV without explicit regime switching, and no sunspot data are used ([Schanner et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12592250/)). ticktack provides a differentiable carbon-cycle box model in JAX with diffrax ([Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.13775)). Aurorae are the weakest proxy before 1800. The main databases conflict, and a Schröder catalogue covering the whole Maunder Minimum contradicts the common compilations ([Stangl & Foelsche 2024](https://doaj.org/article/fab0996c84174f9fa4d9058e8c8f0402)).

**Researchers' derivation.** The forward chain for the proxy-informed variant runs from daily G_t to an open-flux continuity ODE ([Solanki, Schüssler & Fligge 2000](https://doi.org/10.1038/35044027) [nv]), then to modulation φ, to ¹⁴C and ¹⁰Be production, to the ticktack box model and a deposition model, and finally to the measurements. Every link is differentiable, so the whole chain can be sampled with NUTS. Aurorae become a thinned point process with site effort and a catalogue-discrepancy term. Usoskin's conversion from open flux to sunspot number is not applied inside grand minima, so it must be made uncertain or regime-dependent. Schanner's tight scale priors may understate structural error, which argues for model-discrepancy terms ([Kennedy & O'Hagan 2001](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9868.00294) [nv]). Each data type needs an explicit aggregation operator: daily GN, annual ¹⁴C, 22-year ¹⁰Be bins and event-level aurorae. The researchers also caution, without a fetched source, that industrial-era and bomb-era ¹⁴C push calibration back into the era where GN itself is uncertain, which forces joint rather than sequential calibration. No Bayesian model inferring GN jointly from observers plus ¹⁴C, ¹⁰Be and aurorae was found.

### Circularity firewalls and pseudo-proxy benchmarks

LMR acknowledges that its instrumental verification overlaps calibration. It mitigates this by verifying against Berkeley Earth, which was not used in calibration, and against withheld proxies, and its prior carries no time-specific information ([Tardif et al. 2019](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/15/1251/2019/)). Pseudo-proxy experiments separate method error from data error, but translating their results to the real world "must be done cautiously" ([Smerdon 2012](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/wirecc/v3y2012i1p63-77.html)). No single reconstruction method had universally superior spatial error statistics ([Smerdon et al. 2011](https://www.ldeo.columbia.edu/~jsmerdon/papers/2011_grl_smerdonetal.pdf)). Earlier BARCAST tests generated their targets from BARCAST's own equations, a "perfect-model" setup ([Nilsen, Werner & Divine 2018](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/14/947/2018/cp-14-947-2018.pdf)). Usoskin et al. validate against sunspots while relying on a conversion calibrated on sunspots ([arXiv 2103.15112](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.15112.pdf)). Schanner's φ uses no sunspots, so it is an independent target for a sunspot-only GN ([Schanner et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12592250/)).

**Researchers' derivation.** The firewall has four rules. First, the sunspot-only variant admits no isotope or aurora information in its data, priors or hyperpriors, including its grand-minimum prior, and a data-lineage table documents this. Second, the proxy-informed variant serves forcing studies and is never used to validate its own proxies; its leave-one-proxy-type-out siblings are scored against the proxy each one withholds. Third, leave-one-observer-out scoring of the sunspot-only variant is the sunspot analog of withheld proxies, for example with Pareto-smoothed importance-sampling leave-one-out (PSIS-LOO; [Vehtari et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11222-016-9696-4) [nv]). Fourth, three hidden sources of circularity must be ruled out: observer selection tuned to isotope-based Maunder Minimum levels, choosing among Vaquero's readings because one "matches ¹⁴C", and Maunder Minimum priors taken from isotopes. For pseudo-proxy experiments (PPEs), RGO plays the role of the GCM truth. Synthetic observers get real historical calendars, archive degradation, false zeros and date shifts. Maunder-like truths come from thinning RGO 10–100× or from dynamo simulations. Misspecification PPEs, in which data are generated with one model and fitted with another, measure overconfidence, the main failure mode the paper must quantify.

## Simulation-based inference upgrades ADF but cannot carry the shared Sun

### ADF is k-nearest-neighbour ABC, and neural estimators replace it

**Researchers' derivation.** Read as simulation-based inference, the current ADF/EMD method is k-nearest-neighbour approximate Bayesian computation (ABC). Its summary statistic is the active-day-fraction histogram, its distance is the earth mover's distance (EMD), and its acceptance rule keeps the N best matches. The window start date is a hidden nuisance parameter, and the reported uncertainty is the spread of the N best rather than a posterior. There is no regression adjustment and no calibration. The researchers' simulator takes an RGO/Debrecen window, applies a real observing mask, drops groups below a noisy threshold, splits and merges groups, adds drift and deletes zero reports. In JAX it vectorizes to about 10¹⁰ element operations for 10⁶ synthetic careers. That is minutes on a GPU, while training takes hours (both estimates).

The published building blocks are established. Neural posterior estimation (NPE) gives posteriors with no ABC tolerance ([Papamakarios & Murray 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06376) [nv]). Neural likelihood estimation (NLE) learns q(x|θ) for use inside MCMC ([Papamakarios et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07226) [nv]). Neural ratio estimation (NRE) does the same job with a classifier ([Hermans et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.04057) [nv]), and a 2025 guide sets out the practical workflow ([Deistler et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12939)). Summary statistics can be learned as regression estimates of the posterior mean ([Fearnhead & Prangle 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.1112) [nv]) or as infomax statistics ([Chen et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10079) [nv]). Accepted samples can be regression-adjusted ([Beaumont et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1093/genetics/162.4.2025) [nv]), and Simformer handles missing or unstructured data such as irregular observing masks ([Gloeckler et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09636) [nv]).

**Researchers' derivation.** The cheapest upgrade keeps the existing ADF code but adds learned summaries and regression adjustment, which turns its output into proper ABC posteriors. The more valuable upgrade uses NLE to learn an emission likelihood q(y | latent state, θ_o). That likelihood plugs into the forward algorithm, so information across observers is still pooled exactly.

### Hierarchical SBI pools observers but not the shared latent series

Hierarchical neural estimators use parameters shared across units to make local parameters identifiable ([Rodrigues et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.06477) [nv]). Modeling the hierarchy explicitly gives tighter constraints ([Heinrich et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12584)). Amortized multilevel models deliver near-instant posteriors on new datasets ([Habermann et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13230)), and BayesFlow 2.0 lists hierarchical modeling among its capabilities ([BayesFlow 2.0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07098)). Compositional score models combine posteriors from single observations ([Geffner et al. 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/geffner23a.html)), and two-stage hierarchical Bayes recombines unit posteriors fitted under a working prior ([Lunn et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1111/rssc.12007) [nv]).

**Researchers' derivation.** A two-stage recipe supplies the partial pooling that ADF lacks. Train NPE once under a broad prior and draw about 1,000 posterior samples per observer. Then estimate an era-dependent population prior by importance reweighting, at microseconds per evaluation, and reweight each observer's samples to obtain shrunken posteriors. The limitation is structural. Before 1874, observers are coupled only through an unknown latent series of about 1.5×10⁵ dimensions, which hierarchical SBI does not handle. In the reference era, conditioning on RGO makes observers conditionally independent, and that is where NPE works. Exchangeability also breaks for Wolf and his assistants and for compiled records, which need group-level effects.

### Misspecification is the Maunder Minimum problem

Cannon, Ward & Schmon ran the first comprehensive study of neural SBI under misspecification. They found it "can have a profoundly deleterious effect on performance", with no mitigation preventing failure in every case ([Cannon, Ward & Schmon 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.01845)). The prompt's attribution to "Cannon, Ward & Sisson" is flagged in the notes as a mis-attribution.

Mitigations exist. Naive NPE gave "misleading and erratic posteriors", which robust NPE (RNPE) repairs by modeling summary discrepancies ([Ward et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06564)). A robust version of sequential neural likelihood (RSNL) does the same for likelihoods ([Kelly et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13368)). Misspecification can be detected by maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) in a regularized summary space ([Schmitt et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.08866)). RoPE models the simulation-to-reality gap with optimal transport, using a small calibration set of real data with known parameters ([Wehenkel et al. 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/wehenkel25a.html)). Even when correctly specified, every benchmarked SBI algorithm can be overconfident, and ensembles mitigate this ([Hermans et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06581)).

**Researchers' derivation.** The most valuable defence is thinning augmentation: deleting whole reference groups with retention probability drawn from U(0.01, 1) lets the simulator reach Maunder-like activity, though it does not cover shifts in the area distribution. Next comes keeping latent activity likelihood-based, so that low activity is an extrapolation in one scalar parameter rather than an unseen input to a network. Observers whose summaries fall beyond the 99th percentile of the simulation's typical set should be flagged as out of distribution. RNPE-style slack terms then identify which summary is misspecified. RoPE can use observers that overlap RGO as its calibration set, but those observers cannot test extrapolation into the Maunder Minimum.

### Calibration diagnostics and tooling

Simulation-based calibration (SBC) rank histograms check computation ([Talts et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06788) [nv]), and test quantities that depend on the data catch more failures ([Modrák et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.02383) [nv]). TARP tests joint-posterior coverage from samples alone and is necessary and sufficient for accuracy ([Lemos et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03026) [nv]). It also catches estimators that simply return the prior ([arXiv 2512.16869](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.16869)).

## A 41-state forward filter makes the 1610–present posterior tractable

### Exact marginalization of the latent daily count

**Researchers' derivation.** Let N_t ∈ {0, …, K−1} be the count a reference observer would make, with K ≈ 41, or about 60 for observers who split heavily. The transition is P(j | i) = Σ_s Bin(s; i, φ)·Pois(j − s; λ_t), and it factorizes as A_t = S·B_t. S is a fixed lower-triangular survival matrix, and B_t is an upper-triangular Toeplitz birth matrix that depends only on λ_t. Each step is then two matrix-vector products, and the T×K×K tensor is never stored.

Emissions come from per-observer-epoch tables of log P(y | N = k, θ_o). Each record gathers one row, and a `segment_sum` combines them into a T×K log-emission array, about 6.2×10⁶ entries or 25 MB in float32. Records stay in long format; a dense day × observer array would hold about 9×10⁷ mostly empty cells. Two variants share this machinery. The reporting model's missing-zero term enters as an emission. An ADF-consistent variant emits only P(y > 0 | N) = 1 − (1 − q_o)^N. Forward-filter backward-sample (FFBS) draws of N_{1:T}, aggregated to months or years, are the GN posterior ensemble, and fixing the reference observer's detection and splitting resolves the scale.

The cited infrastructure supports this. NumPyro marginalizes discrete latents with `config_enumerate` and `scan`. It needs a dependency graph of low treewidth, and forum threads record shape and broadcast failures when enumeration runs inside `scan` ([NumPyro HMM example](https://num.pyro.ai/en/0.19.0/examples/hmm_enum.html); [forum](https://forum.pyro.ai/t/discretehmcgibbs-enumeration-cannot-detect-any-discrete-latent/5314)). HMM filtering, smoothing and decoding become parallel prefix sums with O(log T) span ([Hassan, Särkkä & García-Fernández 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.05743)). dynamax implements a parallel filter, a smoother that differentiates the log-normalizer, and a parallel posterior sampler, all with T×K×K elements ([dynamax source](https://github.com/probml/dynamax/blob/main/dynamax/hidden_markov_model/parallel_inference.py)). The parallel sampler "starts performing better than serial sampling when the sequence length exceeds a few thousand" ([dynamax PR #342](https://github.com/probml/dynamax/pull/342)).

| Algorithm (T ≈ 1.52×10⁵, K = 41) | Work | Sequential depth | Peak memory | Estimated wall time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sequential `lax.scan`, factorized S·B_t | ≈ 5×10⁸ flops | 1.52×10⁵ | ≈ 25 MB | 0.2–1 s CPU; 1–3 s single-chain GPU |
| Same, vmapped over 256–1,024 chains | × batch | same | 6–25 GB at 1,024 without remat | ≈ same wall time on GPU |
| Full associative scan (dynamax) | ≈ 2×10¹⁰ | 18 levels | ≈ 1 GB float32 per copy | tens of ms on GPU |
| Chunked hybrid (256 chunks of ~600 days) | ≈ 1×10¹⁰ | ~608 | ≈ 2 MB + T×K | 0.05–0.3 s GPU |
| Two area classes (900 states) | ≈ 8×10⁹ | 1.52×10⁵ | ≈ 0.55 GB | several s CPU |
| Three area classes (8,000 states) | ≈ 7×10¹⁰ | 1.52×10⁵ | ≈ 4.9 GB | tens of s; parallel scan infeasible |

Every entry is the researchers' estimate and should be benchmarked early. Day-by-day independent thinning ignores that large groups persist. That makes an observer's detections positively autocorrelated given N_t and narrows the uncertainty on q_o, and the two-class factorial chain is the cheapest exact fix. Summing 10⁶–10⁷ likelihood terms in float32 can shift the total by 0.1–1 nat, so accumulate in float64. It is unverified whether dynamax's parallel filter accepts transitions that vary in time; a hand-written associative operator is about 30 lines.

### Samplers: why blocked Gibbs beats full NUTS here

**Researchers' derivation.** Full joint NUTS over about 1.5–3×10⁴ dimensions would need 255–1,023 gradients per iteration at 1–3 s each, or 4–50 minutes per iteration. That means days to weeks per chain. The recommended production sampler is a blocked Gibbs sweep. It runs parallel FFBS for N given θ, then vmapped NUTS or MALA for each observer's θ_o given N (4–6 dimensions each). Next come per-day draws of survivors and births and a Poisson-log-Gaussian update of λ on a 27-day grid of about 5,600 knots, and finally an update of the population hyperparameters. At 1–3 s per sweep, 10⁴ sweeps take 3–8 hours, and 64–256 chains batched on one GPU take about the same wall time. Mixing is the risk before 1874, where thresholds and the latent level are tightly coupled. Collapsed updates for the worst observers, a joint scale move on all q_o and λ, and split-R̂ checks on yearly GN mitigate it.

Two alternatives have distinct roles. Stochastic variational inference (SVI) on four-year blocks with 100–200-day buffers is valid because buffer bias decays geometrically ([Aicher et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09098) [nv]; [Foti et al. 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.1670) [nv]). It suits development, but its under-dispersed posteriors rule it out for final uncertainty quantification. Minibatching over observers while the latent is marginalized would bias the ELBO. Likelihood-tempered SMC with 1,000–4,000 particles and HMC moves ([Chopin 2002](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/89.3.539) [nv]) takes 2–12 hours. It returns a marginal likelihood for model comparison, and tempering data in backward from the reference era shows how each era's observers move the posterior.

The activity prior should be a non-centred state-space GP ([Hartikainen & Särkkä 2010](https://doi.org/10.1109/MLSP.2010.5589113) [nv]; quasi-periodic forms in [Solin & Särkkä 2014](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v33/solin14.html) [nv]) with a jittered period, because a fixed ~11-year period can impose cycles on the Maunder Minimum. Non-centred observer parameters avoid funnel geometry ([Betancourt & Girolami 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.0906) [nv]; [NumPyro reparam](https://num.pyro.ai/en/stable/reparam.html) [nv]).

A fully continuous daily Poisson or negative-binomial model with a Laplace approximation is a millisecond baseline that can supply initial k-factors. It cannot represent nonlinear thresholds and is least accurate in the mostly-zero Maunder regime. Particle filters that track individual groups, about 64 slots per particle, are a validation tool for selected decades, not the production engine. PMMH over the full record would need 10⁵–10⁶ particles to keep the log-likelihood standard deviation near 1–1.7 ([Doucet et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.1871) [nv]), and the JAX particle ecosystem is thin ([BlackJAX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10797); [smcjax](https://pypi.org/project/smcjax/)). A single-chain sequential scan with 41-vector bodies is latency-bound, so the CPU matches the GPU there. GPUs win only for batched chains or particles and for the chunked or parallel scans.

Two empirical checks underpin the whole engine, and neither has been run. Does an immigration-death chain plus thinning reproduce RGO's daily autocorrelation and its zero fraction as a function of activity? Do grand-minimum groups resemble thinned modern groups?

Ideas 1–3 assemble into a single architecture: a concurrent hierarchical model with a detection layer anchored to the reference, running on an exact HMM engine. Ideas 4 and 7 turn that model into a methods paper that can show where it departs from backbone and ADF series, and why. Idea 5 brings in the Maunder Minimum without letting Hoyt & Schatten's every-day-observed assumption set its level. The benchmark ranks fourth only because it validates the series rather than producing it. For a methods paper it deserves effort comparable to the first three.

The researchers' framing also suggests the paper's narrative: **the existing methods are limiting cases of the new one**.

| Existing method | Limiting case of the joint model |
|---|---|
| ADF | Hard-threshold limit (w_o → 0), with binary-only emissions and days removed completely at random |
| Backbone | Single-path chained linking, with the backbone treated as exact |
| Probability-distribution-matrix calibration | A pairwise Dawid-Skene matrix estimated against a stand-in for truth |

Showing on the blind benchmark what each generalization adds makes a stronger paper than presenting the joint model as a replacement.

Several transfers that look plausible should be avoided. Identifying detection from one observer's autocorrelation, as hidden-INAR models do, is driven entirely by the assumed lag structure ([Bracher 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06688)). Estimating absolute abundance from replicate covariance is biased by shared heterogeneity *(derived)*. A free confusion matrix per observer cannot be identified for sparse observers, and multiplicative BBS-style observer factors carry the k-factor's flaw into a Bayesian model. Gaussian ensemble Kalman updates suit neither counts nor blanket statements. End-to-end SBI throws away cross-observer information, and PMMH over all 151,900 days is infeasible *(derived estimate)*. NumPyro's automatic enumeration across the full scan is fragile ([NumPyro forum](https://forum.pyro.ai/t/discretehmcgibbs-enumeration-cannot-detect-any-discrete-latent/5314)). Chessmetrics-style relative scales give up the absolute count GN needs, and aggressive scalpel-style cutting of careers drains the secular trend into nuisance parameters.

For the two variants, the decisive design element is data lineage rather than modeling. The sunspot-only variant must take its grand-minimum prior, cycle-amplitude priors and population of Maunder Minimum thresholds from sunspot-era data or dynamo physics, never from isotope reconstructions. It should be tested against Schanner-type modulation estimates that use no sunspots. Usoskin-type series are partly circular as targets because their open-flux-to-sunspot conversion was calibrated on sunspots. The proxy-informed variant should be published with its leave-one-proxy-type-out siblings and a per-year map of how far the posterior contracts relative to the prior. That map shows readers where comparisons with isotopes test only the prior: the years for which the sunspot record contains nothing but blanket statements.

## Conclusion

The adjacent literatures move the hard part of the GN problem. Computation is not the bottleneck: by the researchers' estimates, an exact daily posterior over four centuries fits in tens of megabytes and hours of GPU time. What binds are the identifiability limits that current methods leave implicit. They should be published as first-class outputs next to the series. The reference-equivalent target definition fixes the absolute scale. The scale-transfer curve, epoch by epoch, is as unidentifiable as chess rating inflation without slow-drift priors or proxies. The per-year prior contraction and the effective resistance from each observer to the reference show where the sunspot evidence actually speaks. Seen this way, ADF and backbone calibration are not rivals to be replaced but corners of the same model. ADF is Royle-Nichols occupancy observed through the busy-period indicator. Backbone calibration is chained equating run as a forward-only filter. That gives the methods paper a unifying story rather than a horse race.

Two empirical assumptions, not any modeling choice, will decide the Maunder Minimum. One is that the immigration-death-plus-thinning engine reproduces RGO's daily statistics. The other is that grand-minimum groups resemble thinned modern groups drawn from a stationary area distribution. The first can be checked against RGO's autocorrelation and its zero fraction as a function of activity before the full model is built. The second needs solar-physics evidence that lies outside these notes. The sea-level and satellite precedents suggest that modeling sampling and bridging explicitly can shift long-term levels by tens of percent in the sparsest eras. The paper should therefore be designed from the start to attribute any such shift to specific links, statements and priors, using the benchmark's sampling-only and misspecification experiments.
